# How to Create Content Briefs with Surfer SEO in 2026

Your content ranks on page one because someone built a tight brief before writing a single word. That is the real secret behind consistently ranking articles, and [Surfer SEO](https://surferseo.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) makes building those briefs faster than any other tool I have tested.

I have used Surfer on dozens of client projects and my own sites. This guide shows you exactly how to create content briefs that writers can actually follow, without wasting time on guesswork.

Do not skip the keyword validation step. Building a brief around a keyword nobody searches is the most expensive mistake you can make with your time.

## Step-by-Step: How to Create Content Briefs with Surfer SEO

### Step 1: Log Into Surfer and Open Content Editor

Go to [app.surferseo.com](https://surferseo.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) and click **Content Editor** in the left sidebar. This is where your brief gets built. Do not confuse it with the Keyword Research tab, which is a different workflow.

### Step 2: Enter Your Target Keyword and Set Location

Type your primary keyword into the search bar. Set your target country and language. This matters more than most people realize because Surfer pulls competitors from that specific region, not global averages.

Click **Create Content Editor**. Surfer will take 30 to 60 seconds to analyze the top-ranking pages.

### Step 3: Review the Competitor Set

Before touching anything, look at which pages Surfer selected as your competitors. You will see 10 to 20 URLs listed on the right side. Remove any that do not match your content type. If you are writing a blog post, remove e-commerce category pages or Reddit threads from the comparison set.

Getting the competitor set right is the most important step in this entire process. A bad competitor set produces bad recommendations.

### Step 4: Check the Content Score Targets and Word Count

Surfer will show you a recommended word count range and a target Content Score. I typically aim for a score between 70 and 85. Chasing 90 plus often pushes you toward keyword stuffing.

Write down the word count range. This goes directly into your brief as a guideline for your writer.

### Step 5: Pull the NLP Terms and Organize Them by Section

Click the **Terms** tab in the Content Editor panel. You will see a list of NLP-suggested terms with usage recommendations. These are the concepts Surfer wants you to cover based on what top competitors include.

Group related terms manually into clusters. For example, terms like "word count," "heading structure," and "outline" all belong under a section about content structure. This clustering becomes the skeleton of your brief.

### Step 6: Build Your Outline Using the Headings Tab

Click the **Structure** tab (sometimes labeled Headings in older Surfer versions). You will see H2 and H3 patterns from competitor articles. Use this to draft a logical outline.

Do not copy competitor headings word for word. Use the patterns to understand what topics need coverage, then write original headings that fit your angle and brand voice.

### Step 7: Write the Brief Document

Now you move outside of Surfer and into your Google Doc or Notion page. A complete brief includes:

- Target keyword and secondary keywords
- Target audience (be specific, not just "beginners")
- Recommended word count range
- Content Score target
- Full outline with H2s and H3s
- Key NLP terms to include, organized by section
- Tone of voice notes
- Internal links to add
- Call to action at the end

If your writer uses [Jasper](https://jasper.ai/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) or [Writesonic](https://writesonic.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog), paste the Surfer Content Editor link directly into the brief so they can write with the score visible in real time.

### Step 8: Share the Surfer Content Editor Link With Your Writer

In the top right corner of the Content Editor, click **Share**. Surfer generates a link your writer can open without needing their own Surfer account. This is one of the most underused features on the platform.

Your writer can paste their draft directly into the shared editor and watch the Content Score update as they write. It removes the back-and-forth of revision rounds.

### Step 9: Review the Draft Against the Brief

Once your writer returns a draft, open the Surfer Content Editor and paste the content in. Check the score, look at which NLP terms are missing, and flag sections that need expansion. Give specific feedback tied to the brief, not just "add more detail."

This review should take you 15 to 20 minutes per article, not an hour.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Using the wrong competitor set.** Surfer sometimes pulls in pages that rank for a slightly different intent. A 10-minute cleanup of that list saves hours of misaligned content.

**Ignoring word count ranges.** If Surfer says 1,200 to 1,800 words and your writer submits 3,500, the brief failed. Set firm expectations in writing.

**Treating every NLP term as mandatory.** Surfer gives you a list, not a checklist for robots. Some terms will not fit naturally into your content. Skip them. Forcing awkward keyword placement hurts readability and rankings.

**Building the brief after writing starts.** Some teams ask writers to draft first and then check against Surfer. This is backwards. The brief exists to guide writing, not audit it after the fact.

**Not specifying tone of voice.** Surfer tells you what to cover, not how to sound. Without tone guidance, a writer following a perfect Surfer brief can still produce content that sounds nothing like your brand.

## Pro Tips

**Use [SEMrush](https://semrush.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) to validate before you build.** Before spending time in Surfer, confirm the keyword has real volume and manageable difficulty. Surfer does not replace keyword research. It comes after it.

**Create brief templates.** Once you have built five or six briefs, you will notice patterns. Save a master brief template in Notion or Google Docs and fill in the Surfer-specific data each time. This cuts your brief creation time in half.

**Connect Surfer with [Copy.ai](https://copy.ai/referral/chronostrail&utm_source=blog) for faster first drafts.** Copy.ai has workflow features that let you feed your brief as context and generate a structured first draft. The writer then refines inside the Surfer editor. The quality gap between AI-assisted and fully manual drafts has closed significantly in 2026.

**Score at 70 before sending for review, not 85.** A score of 70 means the essential topics are covered. Pushing to 85 or higher during the first draft phase often leads to over-optimization. Polish the score after the content reads well, not before.

**Audit your old content with Surfer before writing new pieces.** Some of your existing articles might rank on page two with a few targeted edits. Run them through the Content Editor before adding more new content to your calendar.
