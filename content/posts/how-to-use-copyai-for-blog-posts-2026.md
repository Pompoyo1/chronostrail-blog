## Step-by-Step: Writing a Blog Post with Copy.ai

### Step 1: Log In and Open the Blog Post Wizard

Go to your Copy.ai dashboard and click "New Project." Select "Blog Post" from the content type menu. The wizard will walk you through the rest of the steps in sequence. Do not use the freeform chat for blog posts. The wizard is faster and more structured.

### Step 2: Enter Your Blog Post Details

You will see three fields: title, keyword, and audience. Fill all three. A weak title like "AI writing tools" will produce a generic output. A specific title like "How to Use Copy.ai to Write Blog Posts in 2026" gives the model something to work with.

For the keyword field, enter your primary keyword exactly as you would target it. For the audience field, write one sentence: "Solo content creators who publish two to five posts per month and want to cut writing time in half."

### Step 3: Choose Your Tone and Set Brand Voice

Click the tone selector and pick the option closest to how you write. I usually pick "Conversational" and then override it with my brand voice note in the next field. This two-layer approach consistently produces cleaner first drafts than relying on the preset alone.

Paste your brand voice sample into the custom instructions box. If you have not written one yet, do it now. It takes four minutes and makes every output better.

### Step 4: Generate Your Outline

Click "Generate Outline." Copy.ai will produce a five to seven section structure based on your title and keyword. Read every heading before moving on.

Delete any heading that does not serve the reader. Add any angle the AI missed. I typically keep four out of six suggested headings and rewrite the rest. This takes about three minutes and is the most important editing step in the entire process.

### Step 5: Expand Each Section

Select a heading and click "Write Section." Copy.ai will generate two to four paragraphs per section. Review each one immediately after generation.

Do not generate all sections back to back and edit at the end. That workflow produces bloated drafts. Generate one section, cut what does not work, then move to the next. You will spend less time editing overall.

### Step 6: Add Your Own Examples and Data

This is where most people fail. Copy.ai cannot pull your personal experience or real-time data. After each section, add at least one sentence that references something you have actually tested, seen, or experienced.

This is also when I add stats from [SEMrush](https://semrush.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) to back up any claims. Fresh data makes the post more credible and more linkable.

### Step 7: Run the SEO Check

Copy.ai has a built-in SEO scorer, but I supplement it with [Surfer SEO](https://surferseo.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) for a more detailed content brief comparison. Surfer tells me exactly which terms I am missing and whether my word count is competitive for the keyword.

Hit a target score before you export. A post that is 80 percent done on content but 60 percent on SEO will underperform a post that hits both.

### Step 8: Export and Final Edit

Export to Google Docs or paste into your CMS. Do a single read-through at normal reading speed. Fix anything that sounds robotic, cut any paragraph that repeats a point already made, and confirm your intro hook is strong.

Total time for a 1,200-word post using this workflow: 25 to 40 minutes.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Accepting the first output without editing.** Copy.ai drafts are starting points. They are good starting points, but they are not finished posts. Every output needs your layer of specificity on top.

**Using the wrong tool for the job.** Copy.ai is strong for structured blog content and short marketing copy. If you are writing long technical guides or deeply researched pillar pages, [Jasper](https://jasper.ai/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) handles those formats better in my experience. Use the right tool for the format.

**Skipping brand voice setup.** I have seen people complain that AI writing sounds generic. In almost every case, they never told the tool how they write. The brand voice field exists for a reason.

**Treating SEO as an afterthought.** Do your keyword research before you open Copy.ai, not after. Retrofitting SEO onto a finished draft is harder and produces worse results than starting with a clear keyword target.

**Publishing without a human read-through.** Every post needs one final pass at normal reading speed. You will catch things no AI checker will flag, including repeated phrases, tonal shifts, and logic gaps.

## Pro Tips

**Build a prompt library.** Save your best-performing title formulas, audience descriptions, and section prompts inside Copy.ai's saved templates. After three months, you will have a reusable system that cuts your setup time to under two minutes per post.

**Batch your outlines.** Spend 30 minutes once a week generating and editing outlines for your next five posts. When you sit down to write, you skip the hardest part and go straight to section expansion.

**Compare outputs across tools.** I occasionally run the same brief through Copy.ai and [Writesonic](https://writesonic.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) to see which produces a better first draft for a specific format. Neither tool wins every time. Having both options saves me from getting stuck.

**Use Copy.ai for repurposing.** After your blog post is live, paste it back into Copy.ai and use the social media templates to generate LinkedIn posts, Twitter threads, and email newsletter summaries. One post becomes five content pieces in about ten minutes.

**Set a word budget per section.** Before expanding, decide how many words each section should be. This prevents the AI from generating 600 words for an intro that should be 150.
