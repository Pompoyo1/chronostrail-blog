## Round 1: Client Reporting and White-Label Features

This is the round that matters most for agencies, and Semrush wins decisively.

Semrush gives you a fully customizable reporting suite where you can slap your agency's logo on everything, set automated report delivery schedules, and build dashboards from scratch using a drag-and-drop interface. Clients see your brand, not Semrush's. That matters when you're charging premium retainer fees.

Moz's reporting is functional but feels like an afterthought. You can generate PDF reports, but the white-label options are thin, and the dashboard customization options are limited compared to what Semrush offers. If client-facing reports are a big part of your agency workflow, Moz will frustrate you.

**Winner: Semrush**

---

## Round 2: Keyword Research Depth

Keyword research is the foundation of every client campaign, and the database size difference here is staggering. Semrush operates with over 25 billion keywords across 142 geographic databases. Moz tops out around 500 million.

When I'm doing research for clients in niche industries or international markets, that gap shows up constantly. Semrush surfaces long-tail opportunities and hyper-local terms that simply don't exist in Moz's index. I've run the same seed keywords through both tools and watched Semrush return two to three times the suggestions.

Moz's Keyword Explorer is well-designed and gives you useful metrics like Priority score and organic CTR estimates. It's not bad. It just can't compete with Semrush's raw volume and breadth. For agencies managing multiple clients across different industries, breadth wins.

**Winner: Semrush**

---

## Round 3: Backlink Analysis

Both tools have invested heavily in their backlink indexes, and this round is the closest of the bunch.

Semrush indexes over 43 trillion backlinks and updates its database more frequently. You get detailed anchor text analysis, toxic link scoring, and a streamlined disavow workflow. For agencies doing link audits as part of a service package, the toxic score feature alone saves hours of manual review.

Moz built its reputation largely on Domain Authority, and DA is still the industry shorthand that clients understand. If a client asks "is this a good link?", pointing to DA is a fast, digestible answer. Moz's Link Explorer is clean and reliable for basic link prospecting.

Here's the honest take: Semrush wins on data volume and freshness, but Moz's DA metric still carries real-world weight in client conversations. I use Semrush for the actual analysis and sometimes show clients Moz DA scores because they've heard of it. That said, for agency work, the analysis depth of Semrush matters more than brand familiarity.

**Winner: Semrush (narrow)**

## Round 4: Local SEO and Multi-Location Clients

If you have clients with physical locations, this round matters a lot.

Semrush has a full local SEO toolkit that covers listing management, Google Business Profile tracking, review monitoring, and local rank tracking by ZIP code. You can manage local SEO for multiple client locations from one interface. When I was onboarding a regional restaurant chain with 12 locations, Semrush handled it without any workflow gymnastics.

Moz does have Moz Local as a standalone product, but it's a separate purchase and a separate login. That fragmentation is annoying in practice. You're already juggling multiple client dashboards. Adding another tool to the stack for basic local functionality creates friction.

For agencies with even a handful of local SEO clients, Semrush's integrated approach is genuinely more efficient.

**Winner: Semrush**

---

## Round 5: Ease of Use and Onboarding

This is where Moz earns some legitimate credit.

Semrush is a powerful platform, but it's also dense. New team members take time to get productive on it. The navigation has improved over the years, but there are still moments where finding a specific feature feels like searching through a cluttered toolbox. If you're onboarding junior SEOs or account managers who are newer to the industry, expect a real learning curve.

Moz is cleaner and more intuitive. The UI is friendlier, the terminology is more accessible, and you can get a new hire up to speed faster. Moz also has a strong educational community through Moz Academy and its blog, which is genuinely useful for training.

If your agency is growing fast and you're constantly onboarding new team members, Moz's lower learning curve has real operational value. It's not enough to change my overall recommendation, but it's worth acknowledging.

**Winner: Moz**
