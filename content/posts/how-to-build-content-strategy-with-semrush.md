## Step-by-Step: How to Build a Content Strategy with Semrush

### Step 1: Run a Competitor Domain Analysis (30 minutes)

Go to Semrush's **Organic Research** tool. Enter the URL of a direct competitor, not a massive media brand. You want someone in your same weight class.

Look at their top organic pages sorted by traffic. This shows you exactly which topics are already driving results in your niche. Export the top 50 pages and save that list.

Now repeat this with two more competitors. You're not copying them. You're identifying what topics the market has already validated.

### Step 2: Build Your Keyword Universe (45 minutes)

Open the **Keyword Magic Tool**. Enter your primary topic, like "content strategy" or "email automation." Set filters for:

- Volume: 100 to 10,000 searches/month
- Keyword difficulty: under 50 for new sites, under 70 if you have domain authority
- Intent: informational and commercial

Export everything. You'll have hundreds of keywords. Don't panic. The next step sorts them.

### Step 3: Cluster Keywords into Pillar Topics (60 minutes)

Group your keyword list into 5 to 8 broad topic buckets. Each bucket becomes a pillar page. The supporting keywords inside each bucket become your cluster articles.

For example, if your niche is content marketing tools, one pillar might be "AI writing tools." Cluster articles under it could be "best AI writers for bloggers," "how to use AI for SEO content," and "Jasper vs Writesonic comparison."

This structure tells Google your site is an authority on a topic, not just a collection of random posts.

### Step 4: Prioritize with the Topic Research Tool (20 minutes)

Open Semrush's **Topic Research** tool. Enter each pillar topic one at a time. It surfaces trending subtopics, related questions, and content angles people are actually searching.

Filter by "Show top 10 subtopics." Pull the questions with the highest search volume. These become your FAQ sections, blog intros, and H2 structures. I use these questions directly as subheadings in my articles.

### Step 5: Audit Existing Content (30 minutes, skip if starting fresh)

If you have existing content, run your domain through Semrush's **Site Audit** and **Position Tracking** tool. Identify pages stuck on page 2 or 3 of Google.

These pages are your quick wins. They already have some authority. A refresh, a better title tag, and added internal links can move them to page 1 faster than publishing something new.

### Step 6: Map a 90-Day Publishing Calendar (45 minutes)

Open a spreadsheet. List your pillars in column A. In column B, put your cluster articles. In column C, add target keywords. In column D, assign publish dates.

Publish pillar pages first. Then support them with 3 to 4 cluster articles over the following weeks. This tells Google your pillar page is the authoritative hub.

Aim for 2 to 4 pieces per week if you have writing support. One piece per week works fine for solo operators.

### Step 7: Use Surfer SEO to Optimize Before Publishing (per article, 20 minutes)

This is where [Surfer SEO](https://surferseo.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) comes in. Paste your draft into the Surfer editor and connect it to your target keyword. Surfer shows you the ideal word count, missing semantic terms, and competitor structure.

I treat Surfer as a final quality check. If my content score is under 70, I add missing terms and flesh out thin sections. This step alone improved my average ranking position significantly.

### Step 8: Track and Iterate Monthly (20 minutes/month)

Set up **Position Tracking** in Semrush for your 20 most important keywords. Check monthly, not daily. You're looking for trends, not instant results.

If a piece drops, check for algorithm updates, competitor content improvements, or missing intent alignment. If it climbs, identify what you did right and repeat it.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Targeting high-volume keywords too early.** A keyword with 50,000 monthly searches is worthless if your site is three months old and competing against Forbes. Target keywords with difficulty scores under 40 until you have real domain authority.

**Ignoring search intent.** If someone searches "best project management tools," they want a comparison list, not a definition paragraph. Match your content format to what Google is already ranking. Check the top 5 results before you write a single word.

**Publishing without internal links.** Every new piece needs links to and from related content. Google uses internal links to understand site structure. Skipping this makes even great content harder to rank.

**Using AI writing without editing.** Tools like [Copy.ai](https://copy.ai/referral/chronotrail&utm_source=blog) and Jasper save hours, but raw AI output often misses specific data, real experience, and original angles. I always rewrite the intro and conclusion myself and add at least one personal observation per article.

**Treating Semrush as a set-it-and-forget-it tool.** Your keyword universe needs refreshing every 90 days. Search trends shift. New competitors emerge. What worked six months ago may be saturated today.

## Pro Tips

**Use Semrush's Keyword Gap tool.** Enter your domain alongside two competitors. It shows keywords they rank for that you don't. This is the fastest way to find content gaps you can fill immediately.

**Build a "question hub" article for each pillar.** Find 15 to 20 common questions in your niche using Topic Research and the People Also Ask data in Semrush. Answer all of them in one long-form piece. These articles attract featured snippets and voice search traffic.

**Pair Semrush with Surfer for full coverage.** Semrush tells you what to write about. Surfer tells you how to write it so it outranks the competition. They solve different problems and work better together than either does alone.

**Repurpose before you publish new.** Before adding to your 90-day calendar, check if you have existing content that covers a similar topic. Consolidating two weak pieces into one strong piece beats publishing a third weak piece.

**Set a monthly content review day.** Block two hours each month to review ranking changes in Semrush, update position tracking keywords, and identify which pieces need a refresh. Consistency here compounds over time.
