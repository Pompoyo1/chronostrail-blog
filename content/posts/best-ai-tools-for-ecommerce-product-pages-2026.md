## How I Evaluated These Tools

I tested each tool using the same set of inputs: five product types across different categories, a brand voice description, a list of raw specs, and a target keyword. I judged outputs on four criteria.

**Copy quality.** Does it sound like a real person wrote it, or does it feel like a template filled in by a robot? This matters more than most people admit because shoppers feel the difference even if they cannot name it.

**Ecommerce specificity.** Tools that understand the difference between a product page and a blog post produce better results. Generic AI writers treat them the same, which leads to verbose, unconvincing copy.

**SEO capability.** A product page that reads beautifully but ranks on page four is not doing its job. I weighted tools higher if they helped with keyword placement without making the copy feel stuffed.

**Speed and workflow.** Time is real. A tool that produces 80% quality output in two minutes beats a tool that produces 90% output in twenty, at least for most ecommerce operations.

## What Makes Product Page Copy Actually Convert

After testing these tools and reviewing the outputs across dozens of product types, a few patterns became clear.

The best product descriptions lead with the benefit, not the feature. "Keeps your coffee hot for six hours" outperforms "double-walled stainless steel construction" every time, even though the second one sounds more technical and impressive.

Short paragraphs and scannable bullet points win. Shoppers do not read product pages the way they read articles. They scan, they skim, and they make decisions in seconds. AI tools that produce wall-of-text outputs are failing you even if the individual sentences are good.

The right keywords need to appear naturally in the copy. Not stuffed, not forced, just present. Surfer and Semrush help with this step more than any of the pure writing tools.
