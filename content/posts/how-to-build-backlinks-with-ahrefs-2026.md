# How to Build Backlinks with Ahrefs in 2026: A Step-by-Step Link Building Guide

Most sites that rank on page one have one thing in common: a steady stream of quality backlinks pointing at them. I built 47 backlinks in 90 days using Ahrefs as my primary research tool, and my target page jumped from position 19 to position 4. This guide shows you exactly how to replicate that process.

---

## What You Need Before Starting

You need an active Ahrefs account. The Lite plan works fine if you are a solo blogger or freelancer. If you are doing this for a client or an agency, the Standard plan gives you the historical data and batch analysis features that make prospecting much faster.

You also need a cold outreach tool. I use a simple Gmail account paired with a spreadsheet for tracking, but tools like Mailshake or Lemlist will save you hours at scale. Finally, have a content creation workflow ready. If you plan to use content as link bait, tools like [Jasper](https://jasper.ai/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) or [Copy.ai](https://copy.ai/referral/chronotrail&utm_source=blog) can help you produce linkable assets quickly without burning out.

## Step-by-Step: How to Build Backlinks with Ahrefs

### Step 1: Identify Your Strongest Linkable Asset (15 minutes)

Before you touch Ahrefs, decide which page you want to build links to. Pick a page that already has some traffic or rankings. Building links to a brand-new page with zero authority is possible, but starting with a page that has momentum gives you faster results.

If you do not have a strong asset yet, build one. A data study, original research, or a comprehensive comparison post works best. Use [Surfer SEO](https://surferseo.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) to optimize the page before you start outreach.

### Step 2: Run a Competitor Backlink Analysis (20 minutes)

Open Ahrefs Site Explorer and enter your top three competitors, one at a time. Click on "Backlinks" in the left sidebar. Filter by "DoFollow" and set the DR range to 30-80. This removes spammy links and authority sites that will never link to you.

Export the results for each competitor into a spreadsheet. You now have a prospect list of sites that have already shown a willingness to link to content like yours.

### Step 3: Use Content Gap to Find Unlinked Opportunities (20 minutes)

Go to Ahrefs Content Gap tool and enter your domain alongside two or three competitors. Run the analysis and look for keywords where your competitors rank but you do not. These gaps often signal that competitors have picked up links around that topic and you have not.

This step is underused. Most people skip straight to outreach, but the Content Gap tool helps you prioritize which topics to create content around before you prospect.

### Step 4: Filter Prospects by Relevance and Traffic (15 minutes)

Go back to your exported list and open each linking domain in Ahrefs Site Explorer. Check the organic traffic column. Any site getting fewer than 500 monthly visits is usually not worth pursuing. Low traffic often means Google has already discounted the domain.

Focus on sites with real traffic, topical relevance, and a clean backlink profile. I use the "Referring Domains" report to quickly see if a site is building its own links naturally or buying them.

### Step 5: Find the Right Contact (10 minutes per prospect)

Do not email info@ addresses. Go to the actual site, find a real name, and search for their email using Hunter.io or Ahrefs' built-in contact data. Personalized outreach sent to the right person converts at 3-5x the rate of bulk blasts.

Write their name, mention a specific post they published, and explain why your content adds something their readers would actually use. Keep it under 150 words.

### Step 6: Execute the Broken Link Building Method (30 minutes setup)

In Ahrefs Site Explorer, enter a competitor URL and click "Broken Backlinks" in the left menu. This shows every inbound link pointing to a page that no longer exists. That is a problem for the linking site, and it is an opportunity for you.

If you have content that covers the same topic as the broken page, reach out to the linking site and suggest your page as a replacement. This method works because you are doing the site owner a genuine favor.

### Step 7: Track and Follow Up (Ongoing)

Add every outreach email to your tracking spreadsheet with the date sent and status. Follow up once after seven days if you get no response. Do not follow up a third time. Persistence beyond two emails signals desperation and wastes your time.

Use Ahrefs Alerts to set up notifications for any new backlinks pointing to your target page. This tells you which outreach efforts are converting and helps you double down on what works.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Targeting DR 90+ sites right away.** Sites like Forbes or HubSpot are not going to link to you because you sent a cold email. These sites require relationships, PR campaigns, or massive content investments. Start with DR 30-60 sites. You will get more replies and more links.

**Ignoring topical relevance.** A backlink from a high-DR site in a completely unrelated niche does less than you think. Google has gotten much better at understanding context. A DR 45 link from a relevant blog beats a DR 70 link from an unrelated one every time.

**Using generic outreach templates.** I have seen bloggers copy and paste the same template 200 times and wonder why they get a 0.5% reply rate. Personalization does not mean adding someone's first name. It means referencing their actual work.

**Building links to your homepage only.** Homepage links raise domain authority, but they rarely move individual page rankings. Point your links at the specific pages you want to rank.

**Letting your content underperform.** If you send 50 outreach emails and nobody links to you, the problem is usually the content, not the email. Use tools like [Writesonic](https://writesonic.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) to upgrade thin content fast.

## Pro Tips

**Set up Ahrefs Alerts for your competitors' new backlinks.** Every time a competitor earns a link, you get notified. You can then reach out to the same site immediately while the topic is still fresh for the site owner. This is one of the fastest ways to intercept links.

**Use the "Link Intersect" report.** This shows you domains linking to multiple competitors but not to you. These sites are statistically the most likely to link to your content because they have already demonstrated interest in your topic.

**Pair Ahrefs with SEMrush for broader prospecting.** [SEMrush](https://semrush.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) has a slightly different backlink index, which means it sometimes surfaces prospects Ahrefs misses. Running both tools on the same competitor gives you a more complete picture. I use SEMrush specifically for the "Backlink Audit" feature when I need to clean up a client's toxic link profile.

**Write content worth linking to before you ask.** This sounds obvious but most people get it backwards. Outreach converts when your content is genuinely better than what is already ranking. If your page is mediocre, no outreach strategy will fix that.

**Ask for links in context, not just citations.** When pitching a replacement for a broken link or a resource mention, suggest where in the article your link fits. Include the anchor text you want. Site owners are busy, and making it easy for them to say yes dramatically increases your conversion rate.

## Bottom Line

Ahrefs gives you more actionable link prospecting data than any other tool I have used. The broken link method, the Link Intersect report, and the competitor backlink export are the three workflows that move the needle fastest. You do not need hundreds of outreach emails. You need the right 30 prospects, a legitimate reason to reach out, and content that earns the link.

Start with Step 2 today. Export your top competitor's backlinks and spend 20 minutes filtering the list. That one action gives you a real prospect list before noon.
