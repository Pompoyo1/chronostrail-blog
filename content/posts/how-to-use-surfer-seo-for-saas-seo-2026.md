## Step-by-Step Walkthrough

### Step 1: Build Your Keyword Clusters in Surfer's Keyword Research Tool

Open Surfer and navigate to Keyword Research. Enter your seed keyword. For SaaS, this might be something like "project management software for agencies" or "CRM for small business."

Surfer groups related keywords into topical clusters automatically. Export the clusters that match your ICP and funnel stage. Focus on clusters with a mix of informational and commercial intent.

### Step 2: Prioritize by Topical Authority, Not Just Volume

Most teams make the mistake of chasing high-volume keywords first. That's the wrong move for a SaaS site without existing domain authority.

Pick one cluster and commit to dominating it before moving to the next. Surfer's Content Score metric helps here. A cluster where competing pages score between 50 and 65 is easier to break into than one where every page scores above 75.

### Step 3: Run a SERP Analysis on Your Target Keyword

Click into SERP Analyzer for your primary keyword. Surfer shows you the top-ranking pages, their word counts, heading structures, and keyword usage patterns.

Pay attention to the "Common Words" and "NLP Entities" sections. These tell you what Google considers semantically relevant to your topic. For SaaS content, you'll often find that product category terms and use-case language matter more than generic industry terms.

### Step 4: Create a New Content Editor Document

Click "Create Content Editor" and enter your target keyword plus any secondary keywords you identified in Step 1. Set the competitor pages manually if Surfer's auto-selection pulls in irrelevant results. This happens frequently in niche SaaS verticals.

Set your language and region. If you're targeting US-based SMB buyers, make sure the competitors Surfer pulls are US-focused pages, not UK or Australian results.

### Step 5: Outline Your Article Using Surfer's Structure Suggestions

Surfer's outline builder pulls heading patterns from top-ranking pages. Use this as a starting point, not a rigid template. SaaS buyers respond to content that addresses their specific workflow problems, not generic topic overviews.

Add sections that your competitors are missing. If you're writing about CRM software, and no competing page addresses migration from spreadsheets, that's a section worth including. Surfer won't tell you this. You need to bring the SaaS domain knowledge.

### Step 6: Write or Paste Your Draft and Optimize in Real Time

Write your content directly in the Content Editor, or paste a draft you created with [Jasper](https://jasper.ai/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) or [Writesonic](https://writesonic.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog). The Content Score updates in real time as you add or remove content.

Target a Content Score between 68 and 80. Chasing a score above 85 usually leads to keyword stuffing that reads poorly. I've published dozens of SaaS articles scoring in the 70 to 78 range that outranked competitors scoring higher.

### Step 7: Review Keyword Usage Without Over-Optimizing

Surfer flags missing keywords and shows how many times each term should appear. Work through the list methodically. Add missing terms where they fit naturally into the text.

Do not force every suggested keyword into the content. Some suggestions are irrelevant to your specific angle. A Surfer recommendation is a signal, not a mandate.

### Step 8: Audit Existing Content with Surfer's Content Audit Tool

Once you have new content live, use Surfer's Audit feature on your existing pages. Paste the URL, enter the target keyword, and Surfer shows you exactly what's causing the page to underperform.

This is where SaaS companies find fast wins. An existing page ranking in positions 8 to 15 can often be pushed to positions 1 to 5 with targeted content additions and internal linking adjustments.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Treating the Content Score as the goal.** The score is a proxy for optimization quality, not a guarantee of rankings. I've seen SaaS companies publish a 90-point article that never ranked because the page had zero backlinks and no internal links pointing to it.

**Ignoring search intent.** Surfer optimizes your content to match what's already ranking, but if the intent behind a keyword is transactional and you write an educational piece, you won't rank regardless of your score. Verify intent in Semrush before you write.

**Skipping competitor filtering.** Surfer sometimes pulls in forums, aggregator sites, or irrelevant pages as competitors. Always review the competitor set manually and swap in the actual SaaS pages you're competing against.

**Optimizing for one keyword per page.** SaaS content almost always ranks for dozens of related terms. Build your Content Editor with three to five secondary keywords from the start. This is how you capture the full cluster, not just the head term.

**Publishing without internal links.** Surfer won't build your internal linking structure for you. Every new SaaS article needs at least three relevant internal links pointing to it from existing pages. This is non-negotiable for new domains.

## Pro Tips

**Use Surfer's Topical Map feature.** If you're on a plan that includes it, the Topical Map shows you exactly which articles you need to write to establish authority in a subject area. For SaaS, this is the fastest path to building a content moat around your category.

**Combine Surfer with Semrush's Intent Filter.** Pull keywords in [Semrush](https://semrush.com/?via=chronotrail&utm_source=blog), filter by commercial and transactional intent, then bring those keywords into Surfer for optimization. This two-tool workflow consistently outperforms using either tool alone.

**Use AI writing tools inside the Content Editor.** Jasper's Surfer integration is genuinely useful. You can generate a section directly inside the editor and watch the Content Score update immediately. This cuts optimization time significantly compared to a back-and-forth paste workflow.

**Refresh high-performing pages every six months.** SaaS search results shift constantly as competitors publish new content. Set a calendar reminder to re-audit your top 10 pages in Surfer twice a year and update them based on new NLP term suggestions.

**Write for buyers, then optimize for Surfer.** The order matters. Start with what your ICP actually needs to read, then layer in Surfer's recommendations. Doing it in reverse produces content that ranks but doesn't convert.
