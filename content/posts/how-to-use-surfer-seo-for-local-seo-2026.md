# How to Use Surfer SEO for Local SEO in 2026: Step-by-Step Guide

Ranking a local business page in the top three results took me four weeks using Surfer SEO. Before that, I spent months guessing at keyword density and structure. The difference was not better writing. It was having data that told me exactly what Google wanted to see for local searches in my target city.

This guide walks you through every step I use now, including the ones most tutorials skip.

---

## What You Need Before Starting

You cannot skip this setup phase and expect good results. Get these ready before you open Surfer.

**Tools and accounts:**

- A [Surfer SEO account](https://surferseo.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) (the Essential plan works for most local businesses, but the Scale plan gives you more SERP Analyzer credits)
- A [Semrush account](https://semrush.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) for keyword research before you enter Surfer (free trial covers the basics)
- Your target city or service area confirmed in writing
- A Google Business Profile already claimed and verified
- Access to your website's CMS so you can publish optimized content directly

One thing people miss: you need to know your exact service area before keyword research. "Plumber" and "plumber in Austin TX" behave completely differently inside Surfer's Content Editor.

## Step-by-Step Walkthrough

### Step 1: Build Your Local Keyword List First (Outside Surfer)

Open [Semrush](https://semrush.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) and run a keyword overview search for your core service. Add your city name to every query. Look for keywords with local intent signals like "near me," "in [city]," "best [service] [city]."

Export the top 10 to 20 keywords sorted by difficulty. You want low-to-medium difficulty keywords with at least 50 monthly searches. Ignore vanity keywords with 10,000 searches and KD above 70 for now.

### Step 2: Open the Content Editor in Surfer

Log in to Surfer and click "Content Editor" in the left sidebar. Type your primary local keyword into the search bar. For example: "emergency plumber in Austin TX."

Set the location to your target city. This step is critical and most people skip it. Surfer pulls SERP data based on location, so leaving it set to a generic region gives you competitor data that does not match what your local audience sees.

### Step 3: Choose Your Competitors Manually

Surfer auto-selects competitors from the top results. Review that list carefully before accepting it. Remove any national directories like Yelp, Angi, or HomeAdvisor from the competitor set.

You want to compete against actual local service pages, not aggregator sites. Surfer lets you add or remove URLs from the competitor analysis panel. Replace directory listings with local businesses that rank with their own websites.

### Step 4: Read the Content Score Targets

Once your Content Editor loads, look at the panel on the right. You will see a target content score, a word count range, and a list of terms to include. Do not ignore the word count minimum.

For local service pages, Surfer typically recommends 600 to 1,200 words. Local pages do not need to be long blog posts. They need to be complete, structured, and include the right terms at the right frequency.

### Step 5: Write or Optimize Your Page Content

Start writing directly inside Surfer's editor or paste existing content. Watch the green, yellow, and red term indicators on the right panel. Your job is to get terms into the green range without stuffing them artificially.

If writing from scratch feels slow, I use [Jasper](https://jasper.ai/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) to draft a first version, then paste it into Surfer to optimize. You can also try [Writesonic](https://writesonic.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) or [Copy.ai](https://copy.ai/referral/chronostrail&utm_source=blog) for AI-drafted local page content that you then refine inside Surfer. The AI draft gets you 60% of the way there. Surfer closes the rest of the gap.

### Step 6: Add Local Signals Surfer Will Not Tell You to Add

Surfer optimizes for NLP terms and structure. It does not automatically account for local SEO signals. You need to add these manually on top of your Surfer score.

Include your full business name, address, and phone number once on the page. Mention the city name in the H1, at least one H2, and naturally in the body. Add a Google Maps embed. Link to your Google Business Profile. These are not in Surfer's scoring, but they matter for local rankings.

### Step 7: Use the Outline Builder for Location Pages

If you are building multiple location pages, use Surfer's Outline Builder before writing each one. Run the target keyword for each city and generate a unique outline per location.

Do not copy one page and swap city names. Google detects near-duplicate local pages and they rank poorly. Surfer's Outline Builder gives you different H2 structures based on different local SERPs, which naturally creates unique pages without extra effort.

### Step 8: Publish and Track

Publish the page and wait three to seven days before judging results. Go back into Surfer and run a SERP Analyzer for your keyword after two weeks. Compare your page's structure and content score against the current top five results.

Adjust if needed. Local rankings shift more than national ones because local competitors update pages and Google refreshes the local pack frequently.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Using the wrong location setting.** Surfer defaults to a broad region. If you leave it on "United States" for a city-specific keyword, your competitor data is useless. Always set the exact city.

**Keeping directory sites in the competitor set.** Trying to out-optimize Yelp with a service page is a losing strategy. Match your page against comparable local businesses only.

**Chasing a content score of 100.** I shoot for 75 to 85 and stop. Obsessing over the last few points usually means keyword stuffing. A natural, well-structured page at 78 beats a robotic page at 97 every time.

**Ignoring page experience signals.** Surfer scores your text. It does not score page speed, mobile layout, or Core Web Vitals. A high content score on a slow page still ranks poorly.

**Building duplicate location pages.** Swapping city names on copied content is one of the fastest ways to get your local pages ignored by Google. Use the Outline Builder to create genuinely different pages.

## Pro Tips

**Cluster your local keywords inside Surfer.** Use the Keyword Research tool inside Surfer to find keyword clusters around your main local term. Build one optimized page per cluster, not one page per keyword. This prevents cannibalization and gets more pages indexed.

**Run a monthly content audit.** Local SERPs change. Pull your existing local pages into Surfer's Content Editor every 60 days and re-check their scores against current competitors. A page that scored 80 six months ago might need new terms added today.

**Use Surfer with your AI writing tool.** [Jasper](https://jasper.ai/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) has a native Surfer SEO integration. You can optimize while writing without switching tabs. If you are producing multiple location pages, this integration cuts production time roughly in half. [Writesonic](https://writesonic.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) and [Copy.ai](https://copy.ai/referral/chronostrail&utm_source=blog) also work well as standalone drafting tools before you move into Surfer.

**Optimize your meta title and description inside Surfer.** Many users write the page body and forget the SEO meta fields. Surfer's editor shows you character counts and flags keyword presence in your title tag. Include the city name in your meta title. It still influences click-through rate in local results.

**Cross-check your rankings with Semrush.** After publishing, track your local keyword rankings inside [Semrush](https://semrush.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) Position Tracking. Set the location to your city. This gives you week-over-week ranking data so you know which Surfer-optimized pages are gaining ground.
