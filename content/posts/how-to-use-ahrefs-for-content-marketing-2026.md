# How to Use Ahrefs for Content Marketing in 2026 (Full Guide)

Most content teams that use Ahrefs correctly see a 3-5x improvement in organic traffic within six months. The ones that fail are using it like a basic keyword tool when it is actually a full content intelligence platform. This guide shows you exactly how to use Ahrefs for content marketing the right way, step by step.

---

## What You Need Before Starting

You need an active Ahrefs subscription. The Lite plan works for solo bloggers. Teams should start with the Standard plan to get full access to Content Explorer and historical data.

You also need a live website with Google Search Console connected. Without GSC data, you are flying blind on which pages are already close to ranking. Have a content management system ready and a writing workflow in place.

If you plan to pair Ahrefs with an AI writing tool, I recommend [Jasper](https://jasper.ai/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) for long-form drafts or [Writesonic](https://writesonic.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) for faster turnaround. These work well once you pull your keyword targets from Ahrefs.

## Step-by-Step Walkthrough

### Step 1: Audit Your Existing Content First

Before chasing new keywords, pull up Site Explorer and enter your own domain. Go to the Organic Keywords report and filter for positions 5 through 20. These are your low-hanging fruit pages that are almost ranking.

Export that list. You will come back to it later when optimizing existing content. Most marketers skip this step and waste time creating new content when they have pages sitting at position 8 that just need a refresh.

### Step 2: Build Your Core Keyword List with Keywords Explorer

Go to Keywords Explorer and type in three to five seed terms related to your niche. Set the filters to a Keyword Difficulty under 40 and a monthly search volume above 500. This gives you realistic targets that a newer or mid-authority site can actually rank for.

Look at the Traffic Potential column, not just search volume. A keyword with 1,200 monthly searches but a Traffic Potential of 8,000 is a much better target because the top-ranking page captures searches from dozens of related terms.

Save the best keywords to a Keyword List inside Ahrefs. Keep it organized by topic cluster, not just alphabetically.

### Step 3: Analyze the SERP Before Writing Anything

Click on any keyword and scroll down to the SERP overview. Look at the top 10 results and note their Domain Rating, word counts, and content types. If the top results are all listicles, writing a long-form guide will not automatically beat them.

Check the "Also rank for" section. This tells you what other keywords the top-ranking pages pick up, which shapes your content outline before you write a single word.

Pay attention to the Content Gap between your site and competitors. Click Content Gap in Site Explorer, enter two or three competitor domains, and Ahrefs will show you keywords they rank for that you do not. This list is pure content opportunity.

### Step 4: Create a Content Brief Using the Data

Take your target keyword, its top 10 SERP competitors, and the related questions from Ahrefs' "Questions" filter in Keywords Explorer. Build a structured brief that covers the main topic, subtopics, target word count, and internal linking opportunities.

I use [Surfer SEO](https://surferseo.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) alongside Ahrefs at this stage. Ahrefs tells me what keywords to target and what the competitive landscape looks like. Surfer tells me exactly how to structure the content for on-page optimization.

This combination removes almost all guesswork from content creation.

### Step 5: Track Keyword Rankings After Publishing

Once your content is live, add your target keywords to Rank Tracker in Ahrefs. Set it to track weekly. You will start to see movement within two to four weeks for lower-competition terms.

Set up email alerts for significant ranking changes. If a page drops 10 or more positions, that is your signal to update it before competitors lock in their gains.

### Step 6: Build Links Using Ahrefs Link Intersect

Go to Link Intersect under the Backlinks section. Enter your competitors' URLs and find sites that link to them but not to you. These are warm outreach targets because they have already demonstrated willingness to link to content in your niche.

Filter for sites with a Domain Rating above 50 to focus your outreach on links that will actually move your authority. Export the list and run your outreach campaign from there.

### Step 7: Repeat the Content Audit Every Quarter

Content marketing is not a one-time sprint. Every 90 days, pull up your Site Explorer again and check which pages have dropped in rankings. Use Ahrefs' Page Inspect tool to see if competitors have recently updated their content on the same topics.

Update your content with fresh data, expanded sections, and new internal links. Google rewards freshness for informational content, and this step alone can recover traffic you thought was gone.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Chasing volume over intent.** A keyword with 10,000 monthly searches where users want a free tool will never convert for a paid product. Always check the SERP to confirm the search intent matches your business goal.

**Ignoring Traffic Potential.** Search volume is a single keyword signal. Traffic Potential shows the realistic ceiling for a page if it ranks well. Build your content calendar around Traffic Potential, not raw volume.

**Skipping competitor analysis.** If you publish content without checking what the top-ranking pages look like, you are guessing. Ahrefs removes that guesswork entirely, and skipping this step wastes your writing time.

**Not using Content Explorer for topic ideas.** Content Explorer lets you filter articles by organic traffic, referring domains, and publication date. It is one of the best ideation tools in the platform and most people never open it.

**Using Ahrefs without a writing process.** Ahrefs gives you the data. You still need a system to turn that data into published content at scale. Pair it with [Copy.ai](https://copy.ai/referral/chronostrail&utm_source=blog) or another writing tool so research does not bottleneck your publishing schedule.

## Pro Tips

**Use the "Lowest DR" filter in Keywords Explorer.** Sort results to show keywords where at least one page in the top 10 has a Domain Rating under 30. This finds keywords where small sites are already ranking, which means you can compete without a massive backlink profile.

**Set up Ahrefs Alerts for your brand and competitors.** Any time a competitor earns a new backlink or gets mentioned, you get notified. This is one of the fastest ways to find PR opportunities and replicate competitor link-building strategies.

**Cross-reference Ahrefs with Semrush.** I use [Semrush](https://semrush.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) to validate keyword data and check Ahrefs blind spots, especially for paid search trends. No single tool catches everything.

**Build topic clusters, not random posts.** Use Ahrefs' parent topic feature to group keywords under one umbrella. Then write a pillar page and several supporting posts that all link to each other. This is the fastest way to build topical authority with Google.

**Screenshot your baseline rankings before any updates.** When you refresh old content, take a screenshot of current rankings first. This makes it easy to measure the impact of your update and justify the time investment to stakeholders.
