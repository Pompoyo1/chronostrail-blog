## Step-by-Step Walkthrough

### Step 1: Run a Surfer SERP Analyzer on Your Target Keyword

Log into Surfer and navigate to the SERP Analyzer. Enter your primary target keyword. For this example, use "surfer seo competitor gap analysis" if that is the topic you are building content around.

Surfer will crawl the top 20 ranking pages and return data on word count, keyword density, heading structure, NLP terms, and backlink profiles. This is your competitive landscape snapshot.

### Step 2: Identify the Top 5 Ranking Competitors

Inside the SERP Analyzer results, Surfer ranks competitors by their Content Score. Focus on pages scoring above 70. These are the ones Google is rewarding with top positions.

Export this list. You want the URLs, their content scores, and the primary terms Surfer flags as high-importance for each page.

### Step 3: Run Each Competitor URL Through the Content Editor

Open a new Surfer Content Editor document. Add all 5 competitor URLs under the "Competing pages" section. Surfer will now show you which terms appear frequently across those pages, with scores for how often and in what density.

Pay close attention to the "Missing terms" section. These are NLP phrases and entities that top-ranking pages use but that your existing content does not include. This is the core of your gap analysis.

### Step 4: Cross-Reference With SEMrush Keyword Gap Tool

Take your top 3 competitors and run them through the [SEMrush Keyword Gap tool](https://semrush.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog). This shows you keywords those sites rank for in positions 1 to 10 where your site either ranks below position 20 or does not rank at all.

Export the results filtered by "Missing" and "Weak" categories. This gives you a prioritized list of keyword opportunities tied to real traffic volume data.

### Step 5: Build a Gap Matrix

Create a simple spreadsheet. Columns are: keyword, your current position, competitor position, monthly search volume, and Surfer content score opportunity. This becomes your attack list.

Prioritize keywords where competitors rank in positions 1 to 5 and you rank outside the top 20. These represent the highest upside with the least resistance if you create properly optimized content.

### Step 6: Create a Surfer-Optimized Brief for Each Gap Keyword

Back in the Surfer Content Editor, start a new document for each gap keyword. Surfer will generate recommended word count, suggested headings, required NLP terms, and an overall content score target.

Aim for a content score above 80 before publishing. I have tested dozens of articles at 68 to 72 content scores versus 80 plus, and the higher-scored articles consistently rank faster. This is not an opinion. The data backs it.

### Step 7: Write, Optimize, and Publish

Write your content inside the Surfer editor or paste it in from your writing tool. If you use [Writesonic](https://writesonic.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog), it has a Surfer integration that lets you generate and score content in a single workflow, which saves significant time.

Hit publish only when your content score clears 80. Optimize your internal links, add your target keyword in the title, H1, and first 100 words, then submit to Google Search Console for indexing.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Chasing volume without checking difficulty.** A keyword with 10,000 monthly searches that every domain authority 70+ site dominates is not a gap. It is a wall. Filter your gap list by realistic ranking opportunity, not just raw volume.

**Ignoring on-page NLP terms.** Most people use Surfer to check word count and nothing else. The NLP terms Surfer flags are the actual reason pages rank. If your content is missing "search intent," "content optimization," and "keyword clustering" when those terms appear in every top-ranking competitor, you are leaving ranking signals on the table.

**Skipping the content score threshold.** Publishing at a 65 content score because you are in a hurry is a mistake I made repeatedly in 2024. It delayed results by weeks and required rewrites. Set 80 as your minimum and do not move from that standard.

**Analyzing competitors in a different market segment.** If you sell mid-market software and you are analyzing enterprise software sites, your gap data is useless. Keep your competitor list tight and relevant.

**Running the analysis once and never updating it.** Competitor content changes. New players enter the SERP. Run your gap analysis every 90 days on your top 10 target keywords. What was a gap three months ago might now be a cluster of competitor content that requires a different angle.

## Pro Tips

**Layer Surfer with GSC data monthly.** Your Search Console shows you which pages are getting impressions but low clicks. Those pages are ranking but underperforming. Run those URLs through Surfer's Content Editor, find the missing terms, update the content, and watch click-through rates improve within 30 days.

**Use Surfer's Topical Map feature for cluster planning.** Once you identify a gap keyword, Surfer can generate a topical map showing supporting articles you need to build authority in that cluster. This is how you close a gap permanently instead of patching it with one article.

**Combine Jasper with Surfer for speed.** Write your first draft in [Jasper](https://jasper.ai/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) using the SERP data as context, then paste it into Surfer for optimization. This cuts production time by roughly 40 percent without sacrificing quality.

**Score competitor content, not just your own.** Run your competitors' published URLs through Surfer's Content Editor. If their article scoring 85 is outranking your article scoring 72, you now know exactly what you need to do. Close the score gap first, then build links.

**Prioritize low-difficulty gaps with moderate volume.** Keywords in the 500 to 2,000 monthly search range where competitors rank on page 2 are your fastest wins. Build 10 of those before attacking 20,000-volume terms. Early wins compound.
