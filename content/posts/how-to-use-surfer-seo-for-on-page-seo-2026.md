Ranking a 2,000-word article on page one within 60 days is possible when you follow Surfer SEO's on-page recommendations correctly from the start. I have done this repeatedly across multiple niches, and the difference between optimized and unoptimized content is not subtle. This guide walks you through exactly how to use Surfer SEO for on-page SEO without wasting time on steps that do not move the needle.

## Step-by-Step Walkthrough

### Step 1: Run a SERP Analysis for Your Keyword

Log into Surfer and click **SERP Analyzer** from the left menu. Enter your target keyword and select your target country and device type. Let Surfer pull the top-ranking pages.

Look at the top 10 results. Pay attention to word count range, number of headings, and the NLP terms Surfer highlights in green. These data points tell you what Google already rewards for this keyword.

Do not skip this step and jump straight to writing. The SERP analysis gives you the content blueprint before you write a single word.

### Step 2: Create a Content Editor Document

Click **Content Editor** in the left sidebar and hit **Create New Document**. Enter your exact target keyword. Choose the competitor pages Surfer will analyze, typically the top 10, but you can deselect irrelevant pages like Reddit threads or forums that would skew your data.

Surfer generates a content score target, a word count range, a recommended number of headings, images, and paragraphs. Screenshot or copy these targets before writing anything. They are your benchmark.

### Step 3: Set Your Content Score Target

Surfer gives every document a content score from 0 to 100. I aim for a minimum score of 70 before publishing, but 80 or above is where I see consistent ranking results. Do not obsess over hitting 100. Forcing every keyword into your content damages readability and signals over-optimization.

The score updates in real time as you write or paste content. Use it as a live feedback loop, not a final grade to chase blindly.

### Step 4: Use the Outline Builder

Click the **Outline** tab inside the Content Editor. Surfer shows you common headings and questions used by top-ranking pages. Select the headings that match your article structure and insert them directly into your document.

This is the feature most people ignore and it is one of the most valuable. The Outline Builder saves 30 minutes of manual competitor research per article. Pick headings that match real user intent, not just keyword-stuffed H2s.

### Step 5: Write or Paste Your Content

Write directly in the editor or paste your draft. I write first drafts in [Jasper](https://jasper.ai/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) using a content brief based on my SERP analysis, then paste into Surfer for scoring. Alternatively, [Writesonic](https://writesonic.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) integrates with Surfer natively if you want AI output scored in real time.

Watch the right sidebar as you write. Surfer shows which NLP terms appear in green (used enough) and which ones are missing or underused in yellow and red.

### Step 6: Hit Your NLP Term Targets

This is the core of how to use Surfer SEO for on-page SEO effectively. The NLP terms panel shows exact phrases that top competitors use. Your job is to include them naturally, not to stuff them in awkward places.

Focus first on red terms, which are missing entirely. Then address yellow terms that need more mentions. Green terms are already covered. Work through the list methodically until the majority of terms shift to green.

### Step 7: Optimize Headings, Images, and Paragraphs

Surfer recommends a specific number of headings, images, and paragraphs. Check these targets in the top bar of the Content Editor. If your article needs 12 headings and you have 7, add more structured sections. If you need 8 images and you have 2, add relevant screenshots or graphics.

These structural recommendations are based on what already ranks. Following them is not optional if you want to compete. Treat them as hard requirements.

### Step 8: Run the Audit for Existing Pages

If you are optimizing a published article, use the **Audit** feature instead of the Content Editor. Enter your URL and target keyword. Surfer pulls the live page and scores it against current SERP competitors.

The Audit shows missing terms, structural gaps, page speed notes, and internal linking suggestions. Work through the recommendations from highest priority to lowest. In my experience, fixing the top five audit recommendations alone improves rankings within three to four weeks.

### Step 9: Export and Publish

Once your content score hits your target and your NLP terms are mostly green, export the document as a Google Doc or copy the HTML directly. Paste it into your CMS, add your internal links, and publish.

Do not hold the article for weeks of additional polishing. Publish it, let Google index it, and revisit in 30 days using the Audit tool to make incremental improvements.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Chasing a perfect 100 content score.** Over-inserting keywords to hit 100 makes content read like it was written by a machine. Google's quality raters can spot this. A score of 75 to 85 with natural writing outperforms a score of 98 with forced phrases every time.

**Ignoring the structural recommendations.** Writers often fix the NLP terms and ignore the heading count or image count. Surfer analyzes the full page structure, not just keyword density. Both matter.

**Using Surfer without checking search intent first.** Surfer tells you how to optimize for a keyword. It does not always tell you if you are targeting the wrong keyword. Validate search intent manually before building your content brief.

**Optimizing once and never returning.** SERPs change. Competitors update their pages. Check your important articles in the Audit tool every 60 to 90 days and update based on fresh competitor data.

**Skipping competitor deselection.** If Reddit, Wikipedia, or YouTube ranks for your keyword, deselect those pages when setting up your Content Editor document. Their structure and word counts will skew your targets in ways that do not apply to a standard blog post.

**Re-optimize before building links.** Many people build backlinks to underperforming content before fixing on-page issues. Get your Surfer score to 75 or above first. Links push already-optimized pages. They rarely rescue poorly optimized ones.
