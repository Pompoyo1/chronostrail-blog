# How to Use Writesonic for Ads: Create High-Converting Ad Copy Fast

Your Google ad clicks double when the headline matches what your audience already wants to hear. That is the whole game, and [Writesonic](https://writesonic.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) makes it significantly easier to get there without staring at a blank doc for two hours.

I have run ads for e-commerce brands, SaaS products, and local service businesses. Writesonic is the tool I keep coming back to for fast first drafts that actually sound like they were written by someone who understands conversion. Here is exactly how to use it.

---

## What You Need Before Starting

You do not need a copywriting background. You do need a few things lined up before you open Writesonic.

- **A Writesonic account.** The free plan gives you limited credits. The Individual plan at $20/month covers most small business ad needs. Sign up at [writesonic.com](https://writesonic.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog).
- **Your product or service description.** One to three sentences. Be specific. "We sell running shoes" will give you garbage output. "We sell lightweight trail running shoes for women who run 5K to half marathon distances" will give you usable copy.
- **Your target audience.** Age range, pain points, what they have already tried. The more specific, the better your output.
- **The ad platform.** Know whether you are writing for Google Search, Facebook, Instagram, or LinkedIn. Character limits and tone differ across all of them.
- **Competitor research (optional but recommended).** Run a quick search on [SEMrush](https://semrush.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) to see what your competitors are bidding on. Knowing their angles helps you write ads that stand out instead of blending in.

## Step-by-Step Walkthrough

### Step 1: Log In and Navigate to the Ad Copy Tool

Log into your Writesonic account. From the dashboard, click on **"Templates"** in the left sidebar. Type "ad" in the search bar. You will see options like Google Ads, Facebook Ads, and LinkedIn Ads. Select the one that matches your platform.

### Step 2: Fill In the Product Description Field

This is the most important input. Do not rush it. Write a tight description that includes your product name, its primary benefit, and who it is for.

Bad example: "Project management software."
Good example: "Teamflow is a project management app for remote teams of 5 to 50 people that replaces Slack, Trello, and Asana with one unified workspace."

The quality of your output is almost entirely dependent on this field.

### Step 3: Define Your Target Audience

There is a dedicated audience field in most Writesonic ad templates. Fill it in. "Small business owners" is too broad. "Freelance graphic designers who manage client projects and struggle with invoice tracking" gives the AI something real to work with.

### Step 4: Add Keywords

Enter your primary keywords. If you have done keyword research on [SEMrush](https://semrush.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) or [Surfer](https://surferseo.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog), pull your top three to five terms and drop them in. Writesonic will weave them into the copy naturally rather than forcing them in awkwardly.

### Step 5: Select Tone and Generate

Choose a tone from the dropdown. For most ads, "Persuasive" or "Excited" works well. Click **Generate**. Writesonic will produce multiple variations, usually four to six, depending on your plan.

Read all of them. Do not just grab the first one.

### Step 6: Edit and Refine the Output

Pick the strongest variation. Then edit it yourself. Check character limits for your platform (Google Search headlines cap at 30 characters, descriptions at 90). Tighten loose phrasing. Make sure the CTA is specific, "Start your free trial" beats "Learn more" every single time.

### Step 7: Test Multiple Variations

Take your top two or three outputs and run them as A/B tests. Writesonic gives you enough variations to test without extra work on your end. This is where most people leave money on the table by picking one ad and never testing.

### Step 8: Use Chatsonic for Refinements

If none of the outputs are quite right, open Chatsonic inside Writesonic. Paste the best variation and ask it to rewrite the headline with a stronger urgency angle, or to shorten the description by 15 characters. It handles targeted edits well.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Using vague product descriptions.** I see this constantly. People type three generic words and wonder why the output is useless. The AI reflects exactly what you give it. Garbage in, garbage out.

**Skipping the audience field.** This field changes the emotional angle of the copy. Writesonic writes differently for "budget-conscious college students" versus "enterprise HR managers." Do not leave it blank.

**Accepting the first output without reading all variations.** The fourth or fifth variation is often the sharpest. Read everything before you decide.

**Ignoring character limits.** Google will truncate your headline if it runs over 30 characters. Always paste your final copy into a character counter before it goes live. Writesonic does not always hit exact limits automatically.

**Not editing the output at all.** Writesonic is a starting point, not a finished product. Every ad I have launched needed at least one round of human editing before it was truly ready.

**Skipping A/B testing.** Running a single ad version and drawing conclusions from it is a waste of budget. Writesonic gives you multiple variations for a reason.

## Pro Tips

**Combine Writesonic with keyword data from SEMrush.** Before I generate anything, I run a quick competitor analysis on [SEMrush](https://semrush.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) to find high-intent keywords and check what angles competitors are using. That research sharpens my Writesonic inputs dramatically.

**Use the "Landing Page" template to match your ad copy.** One of the biggest reasons ads underperform is message mismatch between the ad and the landing page. Writesonic has a landing page generator. Use it after you finalize your ad copy so the messaging stays consistent from click to conversion.

**Try Copy.ai for a second opinion.** I am not loyal to one tool. When a Writesonic output is not clicking, I paste the same inputs into [Copy.ai](https://copy.ai/referral/chronostrail&utm_source=blog) and see if a different engine produces a stronger angle. Both tools have different creative tendencies, and comparing outputs takes five minutes.

**Generate ads in batches.** Instead of writing one ad for one campaign, generate ads for three to five variations at once. It takes the same amount of setup time and gives you a full testing library for weeks.

**Save your best-performing prompts.** Once you find a product description format that produces strong output, save it somewhere. I keep a running doc of proven prompt structures organized by industry. It cuts my Writesonic setup time in half on repeat projects.

**If you need long-form copy to support your ads, use Jasper.** [Jasper](https://jasper.ai/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) handles deeper content like advertorials and long-form sales pages better than Writesonic does. I use Writesonic for the short-form ad units and Jasper when I need the supporting content ecosystem around the campaign.

**Pair ad copy with SEO content using Surfer.** If you are running ads to a content page rather than a product page, make sure that page is optimized. [Surfer](https://surferseo.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) will tell you exactly what your page needs to rank and convert. Better organic signals also tend to improve your Quality Score on Google.
