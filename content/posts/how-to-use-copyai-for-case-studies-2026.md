## Step-by-Step Walkthrough

### Step 1: Organize Your Source Data Into a Brief Document

Before touching Copy.ai, take your raw interview notes and compress them into a single brief. Write out the customer name, their industry, the problem they had before your product, the specific actions they took, and the measurable results they got. Keep this document to 300-500 words. You will paste sections of this into your prompts.

### Step 2: Open Copy.ai and Navigate to Workflows

Log in to Copy.ai and go to the Workflows section. This is where the real time savings happen. Do not use the basic chat interface for case studies. Workflows let you chain multiple outputs together so you are not manually copy-pasting between prompts.

### Step 3: Select or Build a Long-Form Content Workflow

Copy.ai has pre-built workflow templates. Search for "case study" or "long-form article." If you do not find one you like, build a simple three-step workflow: input brief, generate section drafts, compile final output. This takes about 10 minutes to set up once, and you reuse it every time.

### Step 4: Input Your Brief Into the First Workflow Step

Paste your 300-500 word brief into the first input field. Add a prompt instruction that tells Copy.ai the tone and audience. Something like: "Write for a B2B buyer who is skeptical and needs proof, not hype. Use specific numbers where provided. Avoid marketing fluff." Specific instructions produce dramatically better outputs than generic ones.

### Step 5: Generate the Challenge and Background Section

Run the first generation step. Copy.ai will draft the opening section. Read it immediately and do not accept it at face value. Check that every claim it makes is sourced from your brief. Delete anything it invented or generalized. This review step takes five minutes but prevents embarrassing errors in published case studies.

### Step 6: Generate the Solution Section

Move to the next workflow step and prompt Copy.ai to explain how your product or service addressed the customer's challenge. Provide bullet points from your brief about specific features used or actions taken. The more granular your input, the more specific the output. Vague prompts produce vague copy.

### Step 7: Generate the Results Section

This is the most important section and the one buyers read first. Paste in every hard number from your brief. Prompt Copy.ai to lead with the strongest metric and structure the remaining results in descending order of impact. Tell it to avoid passive voice and weak qualifiers like "helped" or "contributed to."

### Step 8: Pull and Format the Customer Quote

If you have a direct quote from the interview, paste it into Copy.ai and ask it to suggest a headline for the quote callout. It is good at turning a buried sentence from an interview into a punchy pull quote. You still use the customer's actual words. Copy.ai just helps you identify and frame them.

### Step 9: Write the Title and Summary Using Copy.ai's Output as a Draft

Do not use the AI-generated title without editing it. Run several title variations, pick the structure you like, then rewrite it in your brand voice. I spend about 10 minutes on this step because titles determine whether anyone reads the case study at all.

### Step 10: Edit the Full Draft for Accuracy and Voice

Paste all generated sections into your editing tool. Read the full case study once for accuracy against your source brief. Read it a second time for voice and flow. Copy.ai drafts are starting points, not finished products. Plan for 30-45 minutes of editing on a 1,000-word case study.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Skipping the source brief.** People open Copy.ai and start prompting without organized inputs. The output is generic because the input was generic. Garbage in, garbage out applies here more than anywhere.

**Publishing without a human accuracy check.** Copy.ai will sometimes generate plausible-sounding metrics that are not in your source material. It is filling gaps. Always verify every number and claim against your original interview data.

**Using the basic chat interface instead of Workflows.** The chat interface works fine for short copy. For case studies, you need chained outputs. Workflows save you from manually managing five separate prompts and assembling the results yourself.

**Over-relying on the first draft.** The first output is a scaffold. It needs your editorial judgment, your brand voice, and verification against reality. Treating the first draft as final is the fastest way to publish a case study that feels like every other AI-generated case study.
