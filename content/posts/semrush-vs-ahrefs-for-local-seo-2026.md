# Semrush vs Ahrefs for Local SEO: Which Tool Dominates in 2026?

If you are running local SEO campaigns and trying to decide between Semrush and Ahrefs, I am going to save you weeks of back-and-forth: **Semrush wins for local SEO, and it is not particularly close.** Ahrefs is a brilliant tool for many things, but the moment your work shifts toward Google Business Profiles, local citations, and geo-targeted rank tracking, Semrush pulls ahead with features that Ahrefs simply does not offer. I have used both tools extensively across local campaigns, and this comparison is based on real work, not spec sheets.

## The One-Sentence Answer

Semrush is the better tool for local SEO because it offers a dedicated local SEO toolkit, GBP management features, and listing management that Ahrefs cannot match.

## Round-by-Round Breakdown

### Round 1: Local SEO Features

This is where the comparison is basically over before it starts. [Semrush](https://semrush.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) has a full Local SEO toolkit that includes listing management across dozens of directories, Google Business Profile management, review monitoring, and local rank tracking with neighborhood-level precision.

Ahrefs has none of that. You can do keyword research and backlink work for a local site inside Ahrefs, but there is no GBP integration, no citation tracking, and no listing distribution. If your client's local citations are inconsistent across the web, Ahrefs will not help you find or fix that.

**Winner: Semrush, by a wide margin.**

---

### Round 2: Local Keyword Research

Both tools do solid keyword research, but I find Semrush more useful for local queries. The Keyword Magic Tool in Semrush lets you filter by keyword intent and shows search volume at the city level in many regions, which matters when you are trying to rank a plumber in Austin versus Dallas.

Ahrefs has strong keyword data and I genuinely respect its Keywords Explorer, but its local granularity is limited. You can target a country, and sometimes a region, but city-level volume data is inconsistent and the tool does not prompt you to think in local terms the way Semrush does.

For content strategy layered on top of your keyword work, I have used tools like [Surfer SEO](https://surferseo.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) to build out locally-optimized pages once the keyword list is finalized. That combination of Semrush plus Surfer is genuinely powerful for local content.

**Winner: Semrush.**

---

### Round 3: Backlink Analysis

This is the one round Ahrefs wins, and I want to be honest about it. Ahrefs has the most comprehensive backlink index I have used. The data is fresh, the interface is clean, and the Site Explorer tool makes it easy to analyze competitor link profiles in detail.

Semrush's backlink analysis is solid. I have no complaints using it for local campaigns where you are mostly looking at local directories, news sites, and chamber of commerce links. But if a client asks me to do a deep competitor backlink audit, I often open Ahrefs because I trust the data more.

For pure local SEO work, the difference matters less than it would for a national campaign. Local link profiles are smaller and the gap between the tools narrows. But Ahrefs still wins this round cleanly.

**Winner: Ahrefs.**

---

### Round 4: Rank Tracking for Local Campaigns

Semrush's Position Tracking tool lets you set a specific ZIP code or city, then track rankings for keywords in that precise location. You can monitor rankings as if you are physically searching from a specific neighborhood. That is exactly what you need when managing local clients.

Ahrefs' Rank Tracker does the job for standard campaigns, but the local targeting is not as granular. You are mostly working at the country or state level. For a national brand, that is fine. For a local roofing company trying to dominate a specific metro area, you need more precision than Ahrefs offers.

I have run campaigns where Google was showing drastically different local pack results just a few miles apart. Without ZIP-code-level tracking, you are flying partially blind. Semrush solves that problem. Ahrefs does not.

**Winner: Semrush.**

---

### Round 5: Reporting and Client Management

If you are running an agency and reporting to local business clients, Semrush is the better tool here too. The white-label reporting features, combined with the ability to pull GBP data, ranking data, and listing audit data into a single report, make client communication much easier.

Ahrefs does have reporting features and they work well, but the local-specific data simply is not there to pull from. Your Ahrefs reports will cover rankings and backlinks, but you cannot include citation health or GBP performance because Ahrefs does not track those things.

For agencies producing AI-assisted content on top of their SEO work, I have found tools like [Jasper](https://jasper.ai/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) and [Writesonic](https://writesonic.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) useful for scaling local landing pages without ballooning production time. Pairing those with a Semrush workflow keeps everything tidy.

**Winner: Semrush.**

## Right Tool for the Right Job

**Choose Semrush if:**
- You are running local SEO campaigns for one or more clients
- You need GBP integration and review monitoring in your workflow
- You want listing management and citation distribution without paying for a separate tool like BrightLocal
- You are tracking rankings at the city or ZIP code level
- You run an agency and need local-specific client reports

**Choose Ahrefs if:**
- Backlink analysis is the core of your SEO work
- You are focused on national or international SEO more than local
- You prefer a cleaner, more minimal interface
- You want the most accurate backlink data available
- Local SEO is a small part of what you do, not the main focus

I want to be clear: Ahrefs is not a bad tool. I still use it for certain tasks. If a local SEO client has a serious link building problem and I need to do a deep competitor analysis, I will open Ahrefs. But my primary tool for local campaigns is Semrush, and that has not changed.

There is also a case for using both. If budget allows, running Semrush as your local SEO hub and Ahrefs as your link research tool covers every base. For content production layered on top, [Copy.ai](https://copy.ai/referral/chronostrail&utm_source=blog) has been useful for scaling location-specific copy without writing every variation from scratch.

[Semrush](https://semrush.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) is the better tool for local SEO in 2026, and I would recommend it without hesitation to anyone whose primary focus is helping local businesses rank.
