My last landing page written with [Jasper AI](https://jasper.ai/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) converted at 11.3%. The one before it, written manually, sat at 3.8%. That gap is not a coincidence.

Landing pages live and die by their copy. If you are still writing every word from scratch or outsourcing to expensive copywriters, you are leaving money on the table. ## What You Need Before Starting

You cannot write great landing pages with AI if you skip the prep work. Most people do, which is why their AI-generated copy sounds generic.

**Here is what you need ready:**

- A [Jasper AI account](https://jasper.ai/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) (the Creator plan works for solo operators, the Pro plan is better if you are building multiple pages)
- A clear offer: one product, one promise, one audience
- Your customer research: at least 10 real customer reviews or testimonials you can mine for language
- A competitor's landing page you want to outperform
- Optional but powerful: a [Surfer SEO](https://surferseo.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) account to optimize your page for search traffic

Jasper works best when you give it context. The more specific your inputs, the better your outputs. This is not negotiable.

## Step-by-Step Walkthrough

### Step 1: Set Up Your Brand Voice in Jasper (10 minutes)

Log into Jasper and navigate to "Brand Voice" under your account settings. Paste in three to five examples of copy you love, whether from your existing materials or a brand whose tone matches what you want. Jasper will analyze the samples and generate a voice profile.

This step alone separates decent AI copy from copy that sounds like you wrote it. Skip it and everything Jasper produces will feel off-brand.

### Step 2: Define Your Campaign in the Jasper Editor (5 minutes)

Open a new document in the Jasper editor and switch to "Campaign" mode. Fill in your product name, target audience, and primary benefit in the brief section. Be specific: "helps freelance designers close clients faster" beats "marketing software for creatives."

The brief acts as Jasper's instruction set for your entire page. Weak briefs produce weak copy.

### Step 3: Generate Your Headline Variations (15 minutes)

Use the "AIDA Framework" or "PAS Framework" template inside Jasper to generate your headline options. Ask Jasper to produce 10 headline variations focused on your single biggest benefit. Review them, pick the top three, and test the language against your customer research notes.

Your headline is doing 80% of the work on any landing page. Spend real time here. I usually run two rounds of generation and mix lines from different outputs.

### Step 4: Write the Hero Section Copy (20 minutes)

With your headline chosen, use Jasper to draft your subheadline and hero paragraph. Prompt it with something like: "Write a subheadline and two-sentence hero paragraph for [product] that speaks to [specific pain point] and leads into a CTA for [offer]."

Jasper's output here will usually need light editing. Tighten sentences. Cut anything that does not move the reader toward clicking.

### Step 5: Build Out Your Features and Benefits Section (25 minutes)

Paste your product features into the Jasper editor and ask it to rewrite each one as a customer benefit. Features tell. Benefits sell. This reframe is where most DIY landing pages fail.

Use the "Product Description" template for each feature block. Run three to five benefits per product, then arrange them by importance to your buyer.

### Step 6: Generate Social Proof Language (15 minutes)

Take your 10 customer reviews and paste them into Jasper. Ask it to identify recurring themes and rewrite two to three testimonial callouts that highlight specific results. Real testimonials carry weight, but Jasper helps you pull out the most compelling language and format it cleanly.

If you do not have testimonials yet, Jasper can help you draft case study summaries or placeholder social proof framing. Replace those placeholders before launch.

### Step 7: Write Your CTA Section (10 minutes)

Use the "Call to Action" template in Jasper. Input your offer, your primary benefit, and the action you want the visitor to take. Generate five CTA variations and choose the one that feels the most direct and urgent without being desperate.

Weak CTAs hedge. Strong CTAs tell people exactly what happens next.

### Step 8: Optimize for SEO with Surfer (20 minutes)

Paste your full page draft into [Surfer SEO's](https://surferseo.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) Content Editor. Run a content score check against your target keywords. Surfer will tell you what terms to include, what headings to adjust, and how long your page should be to compete in search.

This step matters if organic traffic is part of your landing page strategy. For paid traffic only, you can skip it.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Using Jasper without a brief.** This is the number one mistake. Jasper without direction produces average output. Generic briefs produce generic pages.

**Publishing the first draft.** Jasper is a starting point, not a finished product. Every output needs a human pass for clarity, accuracy, and tone. Plan for editing time.

**Ignoring your customer's language.** Jasper is a writer, not a researcher. If you do not feed it real customer language, it defaults to marketing-speak. Mine reviews, forums, and support tickets before you prompt.

**Writing for everyone.** Landing pages with a vague audience definition convert poorly. If your brief says "small business owners," Jasper will write for everyone and persuade no one. Get specific: "Shopify store owners doing $5k to $20k per month."

**Skipping the SEO layer.** If you want your landing page to pull organic traffic, pair Jasper with [Surfer SEO](https://surferseo.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) or [Semrush](https://semrush.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog). AI-written copy that is not optimized for search is just expensive content sitting in the dark.

## Pro Tips

**Layer in competitor research.** Paste a competitor's headline into Jasper and ask it to generate a stronger alternative. This gives you a direct comparison point and forces Jasper to outperform, not just produce.

**Use the "Explain It To Me Like" prompt.** If your product is technical, ask Jasper to explain it at a fifth-grade level, then at an expert level. The gap between those two outputs often reveals the clearest way to write your hero copy.

**Run A/B tests with Jasper variants.** Generate two completely different headline and hero combinations in one session. Build both into your page builder and split test them. Jasper makes it cheap to test because generating variations takes minutes, not days.

**Try Copy.ai for alternate angles.** I use [Copy.ai](https://copy.ai/referral/chronostrail&utm_source=blog) as a second opinion tool when I am not happy with Jasper's output on a specific section. Different models have different strengths. Use both when the stakes are high.

**Use Writesonic for shorter conversion copy.** For button text, pop-up copy, and micro-CTAs, [Writesonic](https://writesonic.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) produces tight, punchy output that pairs well with the longer-form content Jasper generates. Mixing tools is not a weakness.
