# How to Use Writesonic for B2B Content Marketing in 2026

B2B marketers using [Writesonic](https://writesonic.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) are cutting their content production time by 60% while maintaining the technical depth that decision-makers actually need. I tested it across a full quarter of content campaigns, and the results were consistent enough that I stopped second-guessing the workflow.

This guide walks you through exactly how to set it up, what to do first, and where most people go wrong.

---

## What You Need Before Starting

You cannot skip this setup phase. Rushing into the editor without these pieces in place produces generic output that sounds nothing like your brand.

**Accounts and tools you need:**

- A [Writesonic](https://writesonic.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) account (the Individual or Team plan works; the free tier limits word count too aggressively for real B2B campaigns)
- A keyword research tool like [SEMrush](https://semrush.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) or [Surfer SEO](https://surferseo.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) to validate your topics before writing
- Your brand voice document: tone descriptors, banned phrases, preferred terminology, and two or three example pieces your team already considers "on-brand"
- A clear ICP (Ideal Customer Profile) summary you can paste directly into prompts

If you skip the brand voice document, Writesonic defaults to a generic professional tone. It is functional but forgettable, which is the exact problem B2B content already has too much of.

## Step-by-Step Walkthrough

### Step 1: Set Up Your Brand Voice in Writesonic

Go to Settings, then Brand Voice. Paste in a sample of your existing content, ideally a blog post or case study that performed well. Writesonic analyzes the text and generates a voice profile automatically.

Review the profile it generates. Add any missing elements manually, particularly industry jargon your buyers use and the specific problems you solve. Save it before you open a single template.

### Step 2: Run Keyword Research Before Opening the Editor

This is where most people skip ahead and waste time. Use [SEMrush](https://semrush.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) or [Surfer SEO](https://surferseo.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) to identify your primary keyword, two or three secondary keywords, and the search intent behind each one.

B2B content almost always targets informational or commercial investigation intent. Know which one you are writing for before you generate a single sentence. Informational pieces need depth and explanation. Commercial investigation pieces need comparisons, proof points, and clear differentiation.

### Step 3: Choose the Right Template for B2B Content

Writesonic has over 100 templates. For B2B content specifically, four templates do most of the heavy lifting:

- **Article and Blog Writer** for long-form educational content
- **Landing Page** for solution pages and feature descriptions
- **LinkedIn Post Generator** for distribution and lead nurturing
- **Email Writer** for outbound sequences and nurture campaigns

Avoid the general "Content Rephrase" template for original B2B content. It is built for rewriting, not creating, and the output lacks the structural logic that B2B buyers expect.

### Step 4: Fill In the Prompt Fields With Precision

Open your chosen template. Every field matters. The title field is not a placeholder. Write your actual H1 or a close draft of it. In the keywords field, enter your primary keyword and one secondary keyword.

In the description or brief field, include your ICP, the specific pain point you are addressing, and the outcome the reader should walk away with. The more specific this input is, the less editing you do on the back end. I typically write four to six sentences here rather than one or two.

### Step 5: Generate, Review, and Score

Generate the content. Do not publish the first output. Run it through [Surfer SEO](https://surferseo.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) to check the content score against top-ranking pages for your target keyword.

A score above 70 is a reasonable baseline for most B2B topics. If your score is below that, Surfer will show you exactly which terms to add and where the content is thin. Add the missing depth inside Writesonic or directly in Surfer's editor.

### Step 6: Layer In Human Expertise

Writesonic gives you a solid structural foundation. It does not give you proprietary data, customer quotes, or the kind of specific insight that comes from actually working in an industry. Add at least one original insight per major section.

This could be a stat from your own platform, a quote from a sales call (with permission), or a counterintuitive observation from your own experience. This step is what separates B2B content that gets shared from B2B content that gets ignored.

### Step 7: Distribute Using the LinkedIn and Email Templates

Once the core piece is live, go back into Writesonic and use the LinkedIn Post Generator to create three or four post variations pulling from the article. Use the Email Writer to build a nurture email that links back to the full piece.

One article becomes a week of content across channels. That compounding distribution is where the real ROI shows up for B2B teams.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Using it without a keyword strategy.** Writesonic is a writing tool, not a strategy tool. If you feed it vague topics, it produces vague content. Always pair it with [SEMrush](https://semrush.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) or [Surfer](https://surferseo.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) for direction.

**Publishing the first draft.** The first output is a draft, not a finished piece. Every B2B article needs at least one round of human editing for accuracy, voice consistency, and factual verification. AI tools hallucinate specifics, especially statistics and product features.

**Skipping the Brand Voice setup.** I already said this, but it is worth repeating because almost everyone skips it. Generic tone in B2B content signals to buyers that you do not really understand their world. The Brand Voice feature exists specifically to fix this.

**Using the word count as a quality proxy.** Writesonic can produce 2,000 words in under two minutes. Length is not quality. B2B buyers are busy and skeptical. A focused 900-word piece outperforms a padded 2,000-word piece almost every time.

**Ignoring the comparison tools.** If your content strategy involves any comparison or competitive positioning, Writesonic alone is not enough. Tools like [Jasper](https://jasper.ai/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) or [Copy.ai](https://copy.ai/referral/chronostrail&utm_source=blog) have different strengths for specific content types. Test all three before settling on a single-tool workflow.

## Pro Tips

**Create a prompt library.** After you find prompt structures that consistently produce strong output for your content types, save them in a shared doc. Your team stops reinventing the wheel every time, and output quality becomes more consistent across writers.

**Use the Sonic Editor for iterative editing.** The built-in editor lets you highlight a section and ask Writesonic to rewrite, expand, or simplify it in context. This is significantly faster than copy-pasting between a document and a chat interface. Use it for every piece.

**Batch your content generation.** Instead of opening Writesonic for one article per session, generate five or six pieces in a single sitting using the same brand voice and keyword context. The output is more consistent, and you build a content buffer that keeps your publishing schedule stable even during busy weeks.

**Pair it with Surfer from the start, not at the end.** The best workflow I found is to start with a Surfer content brief, import the target keywords and structure into Writesonic, generate the draft, and then optimize back inside Surfer. This two-tool loop produces SEO-ready content faster than any single-tool approach.

**Run A/B tests on your LinkedIn variations.** Writesonic generates multiple post options from a single article. Post two versions on the same topic one week apart and track which format drives more clicks back to your site. After four or five tests, patterns emerge that make your distribution strategy measurably sharper.
