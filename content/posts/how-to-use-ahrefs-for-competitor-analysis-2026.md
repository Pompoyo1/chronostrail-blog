# How to Use Ahrefs for Competitor Analysis in 2026

Your competitor is ranking on page one for a keyword worth 50,000 monthly visits. You're on page four. Ahrefs will show you exactly why, and exactly what to do about it.

I've used Ahrefs for competitor research across dozens of niches, and it consistently delivers the clearest picture of what's actually working for competing sites. This guide walks you through the exact process I follow, step by step, without any fluff.

---

## What You Need Before Starting

**Ahrefs account.** The Lite plan at $99/month gives you enough data for serious competitor research. If you're on a tight budget, grab their trial first and run through this entire process before your billing starts.

**A list of 3 to 5 real competitors.** These should be sites that rank for the same keywords you're targeting, not just brands you admire. Use Google search for your main keywords and write down who shows up consistently.

**A spreadsheet.** Google Sheets or Excel works fine. You'll be capturing keyword gaps, backlink sources, and content opportunities as you go.

Optional but useful: [Surfer SEO](https://surferseo.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) for content optimization after you identify the keyword gaps, and [Semrush](https://semrush.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) as a secondary data source when you want to cross-reference backlink data.

## Step-by-Step Walkthrough

### Step 1: Run a Site Overview on Your Top Competitor

Go to Ahrefs Site Explorer and paste in your competitor's domain. Review four numbers immediately: Domain Rating (DR), referring domains, organic keywords, and organic traffic.

Don't get intimidated by a high DR. A site with DR 70 and 200 referring domains is more approachable than it looks. Focus on the keyword count and traffic, because those numbers tell you whether they're actually winning in search or just sitting on old authority.

### Step 2: Analyze Their Top Pages by Traffic

Click "Top Pages" in the left sidebar. Sort by organic traffic, not by position. This shows you which content is actually driving visits, not just which pages rank for a single keyword.

Look for patterns. If their top five pages are all "how to" guides targeting long-tail questions, that's a signal about what your audience actually searches for. Screenshot or copy these URLs into your spreadsheet with their estimated monthly traffic.

### Step 3: Pull Their Top Keywords

Click "Organic Keywords" and filter by keyword difficulty (KD) under 30. This isolates the terms where they're winning without massive authority requirements.

Export this list. Sort it by traffic volume in your spreadsheet. You now have a prioritized list of keywords your competitor ranks for that you have a realistic shot at competing against.

### Step 4: Use the Content Gap Tool

This is the most valuable step in the entire process. Go to "Content Gap" under the Competing Domains section and enter two to four of your other competitors alongside the one you're already analyzing.

Ahrefs will surface keywords that multiple competitors rank for but you don't. These are your highest-priority content opportunities because the market has already validated them. Filter by keywords where at least three competitors rank in the top 10.

### Step 5: Audit Their Backlink Profile

Go to "Backlinks" and filter for "dofollow" links only. Sort by Domain Rating of the referring page, highest first.

Look for patterns in where their links come from. Are they getting links from industry roundups? Guest posts? Resource pages? Copy the top 20 referring domains into your spreadsheet. These are your first outreach targets.

### Step 6: Check Their Anchor Text Distribution

Still inside the Backlinks section, click "Anchors" in the sidebar. Look at how their backlinks are distributed across branded, exact-match, and generic anchors.

If 80% of their anchors are branded or generic, that's a natural-looking profile you should aim to replicate. If you see heavy exact-match anchors, they may be taking a risk that you should avoid.

### Step 7: Track Their New and Lost Backlinks

Click "New" and "Lost" under the Backlinks tab and set the date range to the last 90 days. Sites that recently lost links from strong domains may have changed their content or strategy.

Sites that are gaining links rapidly in your niche are doing something worth studying. Note which pieces of content are attracting those new links.

### Step 8: Set Up Competitor Monitoring

In Ahrefs Alerts, set up notifications for your top two competitors. You'll get emails when they earn new backlinks, which means you get notified every time a new link opportunity appears in your niche.

This is hands-down the most underused feature in Ahrefs. Most people run a competitor analysis once and forget about it. Alerts make it continuous.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Targeting keywords by volume alone.** A keyword with 10,000 monthly searches and KD 75 is not a better target than one with 1,500 searches and KD 18. Match your current domain authority to the difficulty scores realistically.

**Ignoring traffic value.** Ahrefs shows "Traffic Value," which estimates what a site's organic traffic would cost in paid ads. A competitor with 20,000 monthly visits and a traffic value of $80,000 is sitting on high-commercial-intent keywords. A competitor with 20,000 visits and $4,000 traffic value is mostly pulling in informational traffic. Know which one you actually want.

**Stopping at one competitor.** Running the Content Gap tool against a single site misses the point entirely. Use at least three competitors to find the keyword overlaps that signal real market demand.

**Skipping the lost links report.** Sites lose links all the time when they delete pages, change URLs, or let content go stale. Those are active link opportunities you can capture right now by offering a better resource to the same referring sites.

**Treating Ahrefs data as exact truth.** Ahrefs traffic estimates are directionally accurate, not precise. Use them to identify patterns and priorities, not to make exact traffic projections.

## Pro Tips

**Combine Ahrefs with AI writing tools to move faster.** Once I have my keyword gap list from Ahrefs, I use [Jasper](https://jasper.ai/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) to draft content briefs and first drafts at scale. The research is the slow part, not the writing. [Copy.ai](https://copy.ai/referral/chronostrail&utm_source=blog) works well for producing variations of meta titles and descriptions for the pages you build out. [Writesonic](https://writesonic.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) handles long-form drafts quickly if you're publishing a high volume of content.

**Filter by "Best by links" on competitor pages.** This shows you which of their pages have attracted the most backlinks, which is different from which pages get the most traffic. These are their most linkable assets, and they tell you what content formats earn links in your niche.

**Look at their broken backlinks.** Under Backlinks, filter for "404" pages on the competitor's site. These are dead pages that still have active links pointing at them. You can build replacement content and reach out to the referring sites with a better option.

**Use the "Positions" history graph.** When you click into a specific keyword in Organic Keywords, Ahrefs shows a ranking history graph. If a competitor's page shot from position 20 to position 3 in 60 days after a content update, that's worth noting. It means freshness signals or a content overhaul moved the needle.

**Export everything to a master spreadsheet before your session ends.** Ahrefs limits exports based on your plan tier. Export your keyword gap results, top pages, and backlink data in one sitting so you can work from the spreadsheet without burning through your monthly credits.

## Bottom Line

Knowing how to use Ahrefs for competitor analysis is not about generating reports. It's about building a clear, prioritized action list: which keywords to target next, which content to create first, and which sites to pitch for backlinks.

The process above takes about 90 minutes for one competitor and gives you months of actionable work. Run it quarterly, keep your Alerts running in the background, and you'll stop guessing about what moves the needle in your niche.

Your competitors have already done the market validation for you. Ahrefs just shows you where to look.
