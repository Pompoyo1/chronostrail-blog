## Step-by-Step Walkthrough

### Step 1: Set Up Your Project and Connect GSC (15 minutes)

Log into Semrush and click "Projects" in the left sidebar. Create a new project using your root domain. When prompted, connect your Google Search Console account.

Once connected, Semrush will pull your existing keyword rankings and surface pages that are losing traffic. This data is your starting audit baseline. Do not skip this step because everything else builds on it.

### Step 2: Run a Full Site Audit (20 minutes setup, 24 hours to complete)

Go to "Site Audit" inside your project dashboard. Set the crawl limit to your full site page count and schedule it to run weekly. Check the boxes for Core Web Vitals, HTTPS issues, and internal linking problems.

When the audit finishes, sort issues by severity and fix "errors" first. For SaaS sites, the most common killers are orphaned pages, broken internal links, and missing meta descriptions on product feature pages. These are not glamorous fixes, but they move rankings faster than most people expect.

### Step 3: Identify Your Keyword Universe (30 minutes)

Go to "Keyword Magic Tool" and type in your primary product category. For example, if you sell project management software, search "project management software." Set filters for keyword difficulty under 60 and monthly search volume over 100.

Export the full list. Then sort by "Intent" and isolate commercial and transactional keywords. These are the ones where people are ready to buy or compare, and they drive pipeline, not just traffic.

### Step 4: Run a Competitor Gap Analysis (20 minutes)

Go to "Keyword Gap" and enter your domain plus your top three competitors. Click "Compare." Semrush will show you keywords competitors rank for that you do not.

Filter this list for keywords with difficulty under 50. These are your fastest wins. Prioritize any keyword where two or more competitors rank in the top 10 because that confirms organic demand exists and the topic converts.

### Step 5: Map Keywords to Pages and Identify Content Gaps (25 minutes)

Open a spreadsheet. List every target keyword and assign it to either an existing page or a new page that needs to be created. Avoid assigning two keywords to the same page unless they share near-identical intent.

Go to "Organic Research" and check your existing pages. Any page ranking between position 11 and 30 is a "quick win" candidate. These pages already have authority and just need better optimization to break into the top 10.

### Step 6: Optimize Existing Pages with the On-Page SEO Checker (20 minutes per page)

Go to "On Page SEO Checker" inside your project. Semrush will pull your existing pages and give each one a set of specific recommendations based on what top-ranking competitors are doing.

Focus on the "Ideas" tab. The tool tells you which semantic keywords to add, how to improve your H1, and where your content is thin compared to competitors. I also run every page through [Surfer SEO](https://surferseo.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) at this stage because its content score gives a more granular view of keyword density and structure.

### Step 7: Build a Content Calendar from Semrush Data (30 minutes)

Go to "Topic Research" and enter your core product theme. Semrush maps out subtopics, trending questions, and related angles. Use this to build a three-month editorial calendar.

Prioritize topics where you have a competitor gap, commercial intent, and difficulty below 50. For each piece of content, write a brief before publishing. Tools like [Copy.ai](https://copy.ai/referral/chronostrail&utm_source=blog) or [Writesonic](https://writesonic.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) work well for drafting SaaS-focused content quickly once your brief is solid.

### Step 8: Track Rankings and Refine Monthly (15 minutes per month)

Go to "Position Tracking" inside your project. Add every target keyword from your list. Set it to track daily.

At the end of each month, filter for keywords that dropped. Cross-reference with your GSC data inside Semrush to find pages with high impressions but low click-through rate. Update titles and meta descriptions on those pages first. That single action consistently improves CTR by 10 to 25 percent in my experience.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Chasing high-volume, high-difficulty keywords too early.** New SaaS sites with low domain authority will not rank for "CRM software" against Salesforce and HubSpot. Start with long-tail, high-intent keywords and build authority from there.

**Ignoring the site audit.** Most SaaS founders run one audit and never look again. Schedule it weekly. Technical issues compound over time and silently kill rankings.

**Treating all traffic as equal.** Semrush shows you volume, but volume without intent alignment is vanity. A keyword with 200 monthly searches and transactional intent is worth more than a 5,000-search informational term for most SaaS businesses.

**Mapping multiple pages to the same keyword.** This creates keyword cannibalization, which Semrush will actually flag for you in the Position Tracking tool. Pay attention to those alerts.

**Skipping the competitor gap step.** This is where the most actionable, fastest wins hide. I have seen SaaS teams spend three months producing content while completely ignoring topics their competitors are already winning with.

## Pro Tips

**Use Semrush's "Backlink Gap" tool the same way you use the keyword gap.** Find domains linking to competitors but not to you, then prioritize outreach to those sites. This is the fastest way to build topical authority in a specific SaaS category.

**Set up Semrush alerts for your competitors.** Go to their domain in Organic Research, click "Changes," and Semrush will email you when they publish new pages that start ranking. You get early warning on content gaps before they widen.

**Combine Semrush keyword data with Surfer SEO briefs.** Semrush tells you what to target. [Surfer](https://surferseo.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) tells you exactly how to structure and write the page to outrank what is already there. Using both together is the single biggest upgrade I made to my SaaS content workflow.

**Filter by keyword difficulty under 30 for your first 90 days.** This is not permanent, but it accelerates early wins that build domain authority and team confidence. Low difficulty keywords in SaaS often include feature-specific and integration-specific queries, and those convert extremely well.

**Use the Content Audit tool to find zombie pages.** These are old posts getting zero traffic and zero links. Either update them with fresh data or redirect them to stronger pages. Zombie pages dilute crawl budget and pull down overall site authority.
