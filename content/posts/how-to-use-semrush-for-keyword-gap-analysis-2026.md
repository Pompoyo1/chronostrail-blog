# How to Use Semrush for Keyword Gap Analysis in 2026 (Step-by-Step)

Your competitors are ranking for hundreds of keywords you have never targeted, and Semrush will show you exactly which ones. I ran a keyword gap analysis last month on a client site and found 340 low-competition keywords their three closest competitors were ranking for. They were missing every single one. That is what this process does.

This guide walks you through the exact steps to find those gaps, prioritize them, and turn them into content fast.

---

## What You Need Before Starting

You need a [Semrush account](https://semrush.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) at the Guru plan or higher. The free plan limits your results too aggressively to make this useful. A Pro plan works in a pinch but caps competitor comparisons at three domains.

You also need a list of two to five direct competitors. Not industry giants unless you are one. Use sites competing for the same audience and content type as yours.

Have a spreadsheet ready. Google Sheets or Excel both work. You will be exporting keyword lists and you need somewhere to organize and filter them.

---

## Step-by-Step Walkthrough

### Step 1: Open the Keyword Gap Tool

Log into Semrush and go to the left sidebar. Click "Competitive Research," then "Keyword Gap." This tool is specifically built for finding overlaps and misses between your domain and competitors.

Do not confuse this with the Organic Research tool. That shows your own rankings. Keyword Gap is where the comparison happens.

### Step 2: Enter Your Domain and Competitor Domains

In the first field, type your root domain. No "https://" or slashes needed. Then add your competitor domains in the fields below.

You can add up to five competitors on Guru and Business plans. I recommend using at least three for meaningful data. One competitor gives you a narrow view that misses patterns.

### Step 3: Select the Right Settings

Set the intersection filter to "Missing." This shows keywords your competitors rank for that your site does not appear in the top 100 for at all. That is your opportunity list.

Make sure the database matches your target country. If you are targeting US audiences, confirm the dropdown shows "United States." Wrong database settings are one of the most common wasted-effort mistakes I see.

### Step 4: Hit Compare and Review the Initial Results

Click "Compare" and let Semrush generate the results. You will usually see thousands of keywords. Do not get overwhelmed by the volume.

Look at the top of the results. Semrush shows a Venn diagram summarizing overlap. The "Missing" segment is what you care about. Click into that segment if you are not already there.

### Step 5: Filter the Results Down to Actionable Keywords

This is the step most people skip and then wonder why their list is useless. Apply these filters immediately after the results load.

Set KD (Keyword Difficulty) to a maximum of 49 if your site has a Domain Rating under 40. If your site is more established, you can push that to 60. Set the Volume minimum to 100. Anything below that is rarely worth building a full page around.

Also filter by Intent. For most content sites, target "Informational" first. Commercial intent keywords are valuable but harder to convert without strong product pages behind them.

### Step 6: Sort by Competitor Count

Sort the remaining results by the number of competitors ranking. If all five of your competitors rank for a keyword and you do not, that is a strong signal the keyword is worth targeting. Your competitors have validated the traffic.

Prioritize keywords where three or more competitors rank in positions one through ten. That means the topic has proven search demand and is achievable.

### Step 7: Export and Build Your Content List

Export the filtered list to CSV. Open it in your spreadsheet and add three columns: Priority (High, Medium, Low), Content Type (blog post, landing page, comparison page), and Status (Not Started, In Progress, Published).

Assign priority based on a simple rule. High priority goes to keywords that are low difficulty, have volume above 500, and three or more competitors ranking. Medium is everything else that passed your filters. Low is anything you are keeping for future reference.

### Step 8: Create Content for Your Top Keywords

Take your High priority keywords and start building content. This is where tools like [Surfer SEO](https://surferseo.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) become useful. Surfer's content editor analyzes the top-ranking pages and tells you exactly what to include, how long to write, and which related terms to cover.

For first drafts, I use [Jasper](https://jasper.ai/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) or [Writesonic](https://writesonic.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) to speed up production. These tools cut drafting time significantly, though you still need to edit and add your own insights. [Copy.ai](https://copy.ai/referral/chronostrail&utm_source=blog) is another solid option if you prefer a more structured workflow with templates built in.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Using the wrong competitors.** I see this constantly. People enter HubSpot or Neil Patel as competitors when they run a niche site with 20 pages. Use sites at a similar scale. If the competitor has 10x your authority, the gap data reflects their world, not yours.

**Ignoring search intent.** A keyword with 1,000 monthly searches means nothing if the intent is transactional and you do not have a product page to match. Always check the SERP before writing a word.

**Exporting without filtering first.** Exporting 5,000 unfiltered keywords and then trying to sort through them in a spreadsheet wastes hours. Do the filtering in Semrush first. Export only what you will actually use.

**Treating every gap as a content assignment.** Some keywords show up as gaps because your competitors are targeting them and failing. If their pages rank on page three and four for a keyword, that might mean the gap exists because the topic does not convert well or the competition is higher than KD suggests.

**Running the analysis once and forgetting it.** Competitor content strategies change. I rerun keyword gap analysis on active client projects every 60 to 90 days. New gaps appear as competitors publish more content.

## Pro Tips

**Layer Semrush data with Google Search Console.** After exporting your gap list, cross-reference it with GSC data. If you are already getting impressions for a keyword but ranking on page two or three, that is a quick-win optimization target, not a new content build.

**Check the SERP before committing to a keyword.** Click through to the actual search results for your top priority keywords. If the top five results are all major publications or government sites, skip it regardless of what KD says. KD is a rough score, not a guarantee.

**Use the "Weak" filter too, not just "Missing."** The Weak filter shows keywords where you rank but lower than your competitors. These are often faster wins than starting from scratch. A focused content update can close the gap in weeks instead of months.

**Group keywords by topic cluster before writing.** When you export your priority list, you will often find 10 to 20 keywords that are variations of the same topic. Build one comprehensive piece targeting the cluster instead of 15 thin separate pages. This approach consistently outperforms fragmented content.

**Set up a Semrush project for your domain.** Projects unlock Position Tracking, which lets you monitor how your gap keywords move after you publish. Without tracking, you are publishing blind.
