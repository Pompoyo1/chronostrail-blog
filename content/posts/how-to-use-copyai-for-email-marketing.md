# How to Use Copy.ai for Email Marketing: Templates, Tips and Workflows

My last welcome email sequence generated a 47% open rate. I wrote all five emails in under 90 minutes using Copy.ai. If you are spending hours staring at blank drafts or recycling the same tired subject lines, this guide will change how you work.

Learning how to use Copy.ai for email marketing is not complicated, but most people set it up wrong and get generic output that sounds exactly like AI wrote it. I will show you the exact workflow I use, the templates worth your time, and the mistakes that kill your results before you even hit send.

## What You Need Before Starting

You do not need a marketing degree or a copywriting background. You do need a few things in place before you open Copy.ai.

**Accounts and tools:**
- A [Copy.ai account](https://copy.ai/referral/chronostrail&utm_source=blog) (the free plan works to start, but the Pro plan unlocks the workflows that make this worthwhile)
- An email service provider like Mailchimp, ActiveCampaign, or ConvertKit
- A clear picture of your audience: who they are, what problem they have, and what outcome they want
- At least one existing high-performing email you can use as a reference tone

**Optional but useful:**
- [Surfer SEO](https://surferseo.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) if your emails link back to blog content you want to rank
- [SEMrush](https://semrush.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) for understanding what language your audience actually searches

The quality of your output depends almost entirely on how well you brief the AI. Vague input gives you vague emails. Specific input gives you emails you can actually send.

## Step-by-Step Walkthrough

### Step 1: Set Up Your Brand Voice

Go to your Copy.ai dashboard and navigate to **Brand Voice** under settings. This is not optional. Without it, every email sounds like it was written by a chatbot for a generic SaaS company.

Paste in three to five examples of your best-performing emails or copy you have written. Copy.ai will analyze the tone, sentence structure, and style. Give the voice a name you will remember, something like "Casual Authority" or "Direct and Warm."

### Step 2: Choose the Right Template

From the dashboard, click **Tools** and search for email. You will see several options. Here are the three I use most:

- **Email Subject Lines**: Use this first, before you write the body. Subject lines drive opens. Everything else is secondary.
- **Welcome Email**: Strong starting point for onboarding sequences.
- **Marketing Email**: Best for promotional campaigns, product launches, and newsletters.

Do not start with the body copy. Subject lines first, always. Open rates determine whether your email marketing works at all.

### Step 3: Input a Detailed Brief

Click into your chosen template. You will see input fields asking for your product, tone, and audience. Most people write two sentences here. That is why their output is garbage.

Write at least 4 to 6 sentences per field. Include the specific pain point your reader has, the outcome they want, any objections they might have, and one concrete detail about your offer. The more specific your input, the more usable your output.

### Step 4: Generate and Select Variations

Hit generate. Copy.ai will give you multiple variations. Read all of them before you pick one. I usually generate 10 subject line options and pick the two that feel most human, then test them against each other.

For body copy, I rarely use any output verbatim. I use it as a strong first draft and rewrite the opening line and the CTA. Those two elements need your voice more than anything else.

### Step 5: Run the Workflow for Full Sequences

If you are on the Pro plan, go to **Workflows** and find the email sequence workflow. This is where Copy.ai earns its price. You input your product, audience, and sequence goal once, and it generates a full multi-email series.

For a five-email welcome sequence, I add the brand voice I set up in Step 1, set the tone to match, and let it draft all five. Then I spend about 20 minutes editing rather than 3 hours writing from scratch.

### Step 6: Edit for Authenticity

Every email needs at least one personal touch that Copy.ai cannot generate: a specific story, a real result, a client name with permission, or a reference to something happening right now in your niche. Add this in the second paragraph of every email. It is what separates emails people reply to from emails people ignore.

### Step 7: Export and Load Into Your ESP

Copy your finalized email into your email service provider. Check formatting, especially if you use bullet points, since they sometimes break in certain email clients. Send a test email to yourself before scheduling.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Skipping the brand voice setup.** This is the single biggest mistake I see. Without it, every email sounds identical to every other marketer using Copy.ai. The brand voice feature is what makes the output yours.

**Using the output without editing the CTA.** Copy.ai writes safe, generic calls to action. "Click here to learn more" is not a CTA. Rewrite it every time with a specific next step and a reason to take it now.

**Generating one variation and calling it done.** Always generate at least five versions and compare them. The first output is rarely the best one. Copy.ai gets better when you push it for more options.

**Writing a weak brief.** If you type "write a promotional email for my fitness product," you will get a promotional email for a generic fitness product. Write a brief like you are explaining the project to a smart junior copywriter who knows nothing about your business.

**Ignoring subject lines.** I have watched marketers spend 45 minutes perfecting body copy and five seconds on a subject line. Open rates are the multiplier on everything else you do. Spend real time here.

## Pro Tips

**Test Copy.ai against [Jasper](https://jasper.ai/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) for long-form sequences.** I use Copy.ai for subject lines, short promotional emails, and quick campaigns. For longer nurture sequences with detailed storytelling, Jasper handles depth better. They are not competing tools in my workflow. They are complementary.

**Use the "Explain It to a 5th Grader" rewrite.** If an email feels too formal or stiff, paste it back into Copy.ai and ask it to simplify the language. Email copy should read at an 8th grade level or below for maximum engagement.

**Save your best briefs as templates.** Once you write a brief that produces great output, save it in a Google Doc. You will use it again. Over time you build a library of briefs for different campaign types: launches, re-engagement, abandoned cart, welcome sequences.

**Combine Copy.ai with [Writesonic](https://writesonic.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) for A/B testing speed.** I use Writesonic to quickly generate alternate versions of subject lines and preview text when I want to run split tests without spending more time writing. Both tools are fast. Having two perspectives on the same email brief often surfaces angles I would not have considered alone.

**Run your emails through a spam checker before sending.** Copy.ai occasionally produces subject lines that trigger spam filters, especially for promotional campaigns. Tools like Mail-tester.com catch these before they hurt your deliverability.
