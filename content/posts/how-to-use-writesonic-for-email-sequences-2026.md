## Step-by-Step Walkthrough

### Step 1: Log Into Writesonic and Open the AI Article Writer or Email Tools

Log into your Writesonic dashboard. In the left sidebar, look for the "Email" category under templates. You'll see options like "Cold Email," "Follow-Up Email," and "Email Subject Lines." For full sequences, I actually use the **Chatsonic** interface because it handles multi-email outputs better than the individual templates.

Click into Chatsonic. This is your workspace for the entire sequence build.

### Step 2: Write a Sequence Brief Prompt

Do not just type "write a welcome sequence." That produces generic garbage.

Write a structured brief. Here's the format I use:

> "Write a 5-email welcome sequence for [Brand Name]. We sell [product/service] to [target audience]. Our tone is [adjective, adjective, adjective]. The goal of this sequence is [specific outcome]. Email 1 should [specific goal]. Email 2 should [specific goal]. Each email should be 150 to 200 words with a clear CTA."

The more specific you are, the less editing you do later. I usually spend five minutes on this brief and save thirty minutes on revisions.

### Step 3: Generate Email 1 and Review the Output

Hit send on your prompt. Writesonic will generate the full sequence or individual emails depending on how you structured the ask. I prefer asking for one email at a time inside the same chat thread so I can refine as I go.

Read the output critically. Check: Does the subject line create curiosity without being clickbait? Does the body match your tone? Is the CTA specific and singular? If anything feels off, follow up with a refinement prompt like "Rewrite email 1 with a more conversational tone and move the CTA to the second paragraph."

### Step 4: Build Each Email in Sequence, Referencing Prior Emails

This is where most people get lazy and it shows in the final sequence. Before generating email 2, paste in a summary of what email 1 covered. Tell Writesonic what the reader already knows.

This keeps your sequence from feeling repetitive or disconnected. Each email should reference a logical next step from the previous one, not restart from zero.

### Step 5: Generate Subject Line Variations

Once your body copy is done, go back to the Email Subject Lines template in the sidebar. Paste in a summary of each email and generate five to ten subject line options per email.

Pick two per email and run an A/B test in your ESP. Subject lines matter more than body copy in most sequences. Spending ten minutes here pays off immediately.

### Step 6: Edit for Voice and Accuracy

Writesonic is fast, but it does not know your specific product features, pricing, or brand quirks. Read every email out loud. Fix anything that sounds robotic or vague. Add specific numbers, testimonials, or proof points that only you can provide.

This is also the step where I compare against alternatives. I've used [Jasper](https://jasper.ai/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) and [Copy.ai](https://copy.ai/referral/chronostrail&utm_source=blog) for this same workflow. Writesonic is faster for bulk output. Jasper gives more brand control with its memory features. Copy.ai handles short-form hooks better. For full sequences, Writesonic wins on speed.

### Step 7: Load Into Your ESP and Set Delays

Copy each email into your ESP. Set your send delays based on sequence type. Welcome sequences work well with emails 1, 3, 7, 14, and 21 days out. Abandoned cart sequences move faster: 1 hour, 24 hours, 72 hours.

Your copy is done. Now it runs on its own.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Skipping the brief.** Vague prompts produce vague emails. A two-sentence prompt will not give you a usable sequence. Write the brief like you're briefing a junior copywriter who knows nothing about your brand.

**Accepting the first output.** Writesonic's first draft is a starting point. I never use it without at least one round of refinement prompts. Treat the tool like a collaborator, not a vending machine.

**Writing all five emails at once in one giant prompt.** This sounds efficient but produces inconsistent results. The quality drops by email 3 when you batch everything. Do them one at a time inside the same chat thread.

**Forgetting the preview text.** Subject line plus preview text determines your open rate. Writesonic won't automatically give you preview text. Ask for it specifically: "Write a 90-character preview text for this email that complements the subject line without repeating it."

**Ignoring deliverability basics.** Great copy doesn't matter if your emails land in spam. Make sure your ESP is properly configured with SPF, DKIM, and DMARC records before you send anything. Writesonic cannot help you with that.

## Pro Tips

**Use Chatsonic's memory across sessions.** Paste your brand voice notes at the start of every new chat session. Writesonic does not retain context between separate sessions, so this two-minute step keeps everything consistent.

**Research competitor sequences first.** Sign up for two or three competitor email lists. Screenshot their sequences. Then brief Writesonic on what gaps exist in their approach. This gives you a genuine differentiator, not just another generic welcome sequence.

**Pair Writesonic with keyword research for nurture sequences.** If you're building an educational email series tied to organic traffic goals, check [SEMrush](https://semrush.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) for what questions your audience is actually searching. Feed those questions directly into your email brief prompts. Your nurture sequence becomes a search intent map.

**Save your best-performing prompts.** When a prompt produces a strong output, save it in a swipe file. You'll reuse it with minor edits for future clients or campaigns. This compounds your efficiency over time.

**Test long versus short formats.** Writesonic defaults to a medium length. For cold outreach sequences, shorter emails consistently outperform longer ones. For nurture sequences with warm audiences, longer story-driven emails often convert better. Test both and let your data decide.
