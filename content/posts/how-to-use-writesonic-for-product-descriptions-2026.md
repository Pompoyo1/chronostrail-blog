# How to Use Writesonic for Product Descriptions in 2026

Your product descriptions are costing you sales. Weak copy, generic benefits, and zero personality push shoppers to competitors who figured out how to make words convert. I tested [Writesonic](https://writesonic.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) across 200+ product listings last quarter, and the results were clear: the right setup cuts writing time by 80% while producing descriptions that actually rank and convert.

Here is exactly how to do it.

---

## What You Need Before Starting

Before you open Writesonic, get these things ready. Walking in unprepared wastes your credits and produces mediocre output.

**Tools and accounts:**
- A Writesonic account (free trial works for testing, Individual plan for real volume)
- Your product data: name, features, dimensions, materials, target audience, and price point
- A keyword list for each product (I use [Surfer SEO](https://surferseo.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) or [SEMrush](https://semrush.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) to pull the right terms before I write a single word)
- Your brand voice guide, even if it is just three bullet points describing how you sound
- A Google Sheet or Notion doc to store outputs and track which versions you test

The keyword research step is non-negotiable. Writesonic generates great copy, but it cannot read your SEO data. You feed it the keywords, it handles the language.

## Step-by-Step Walkthrough

### Step 1: Set Up Your Brand Voice (5 minutes)

Log into Writesonic and navigate to **Brand Voice** under your account settings. Click "Create New Voice" and paste in 3 to 5 samples of copy that already sounds like your brand. This could be an existing product page, an email, or a social post.

Writesonic analyzes the samples and generates a voice profile. Review it. If something is off, edit the tone descriptors directly. Every description you generate after this will pull from that profile automatically.

### Step 2: Choose the Right Template

Click **AI Writer** in the left sidebar, then search for "Product Description" in the template library. You will see multiple options including short-form, long-form, Amazon-specific, and SEO-optimized versions.

For most e-commerce use, I start with the **SEO Product Description** template. For Amazon listings, use the dedicated Amazon template because the structure matches what the algorithm favors. Do not use the generic short-form template if you need Google rankings. It is not built for that.

### Step 3: Fill In the Product Input Fields

The template opens a form with several fields. Fill each one with care because this is where most people get lazy and tank their results.

**Field-by-field breakdown:**
- **Product Name:** Exact name including model number if applicable
- **Product Description:** 2 to 4 sentences describing what it does and who it is for
- **Key Features:** Paste in 5 to 8 bullet points from your spec sheet
- **Target Audience:** Be specific. "Women 30 to 45 who do trail running" beats "athletes"
- **Keywords:** Drop in your top 3 to 5 terms from your SEO research
- **Tone:** Select from the dropdown or type a custom tone if your brand voice is loaded

Spend 3 minutes here per product. Rushed inputs produce recycled outputs.

### Step 4: Generate and Review Outputs

Hit **Generate**. Writesonic typically returns 3 to 5 variations. Read each one fully before making a decision.

I score each variation on three things: does it include the target keyword naturally, does it communicate the main benefit in the first sentence, and does it sound like my brand. The first variation is rarely the best one. I almost always work from the second or third.

### Step 5: Edit Inside Writesonic Before Exporting

Use the built-in editor to make changes. Do not just copy-paste the raw output into your store. Fix anything that sounds generic, swap in specific numbers or proof points you have, and cut any sentence that does not do real work.

This editing pass usually takes 5 to 8 minutes per description. That is still faster than writing from scratch, and the final output is actually yours.

### Step 6: Run the Copy Through SEO Validation

Before publishing, paste the finished description into [Surfer SEO's](https://surferseo.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) Content Editor and check your score. If you are under 70, add missing keywords or extend a section. This step alone has improved my page rankings significantly across multiple client stores.

### Step 7: Publish and Track Performance

Upload the description to your store. Tag it in your tracking sheet with the date, product ID, and the Writesonic template used. Set a 30-day reminder to check conversion rate and organic impressions.

Testing is not optional. The only way to know if the copy works is to measure it.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Skipping the brand voice setup.** Without it, every description sounds like it came from the same generic AI factory. Your brand has a personality. Make Writesonic use it.

**Using vague audience descriptions.** "Everyone who needs this product" is not an audience. The more specific your input, the more targeted and persuasive the output.

**Publishing the first generation without editing.** Writesonic is a writing partner, not a vending machine. Raw AI output has a predictable rhythm that readers sense even if they cannot name it. Edit before you publish.

**Ignoring the keyword fields.** If you leave keywords blank, the AI writes naturally but not strategically. Your product page will read well and rank for nothing.

**Using the wrong template for your platform.** Amazon's algorithm rewards a different structure than Google does. Picking the wrong template wastes your time and your credits.

## Pro Tips

**Batch your inputs.** Instead of doing one product at a time, build a spreadsheet with all your product data and work through 10 to 20 products in one session. You will get into a rhythm, and the quality stays more consistent.

**Use Writesonic's comparison mode.** After generating outputs, pin your two favorites side by side and read them aloud. The one that sounds more natural when spoken is almost always the better converter.

**Combine tools for maximum output.** I use [Writesonic](https://writesonic.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) for the description body, [Surfer SEO](https://surferseo.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) for optimization scoring, and [SEMrush](https://semrush.com/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) for keyword discovery. Each tool does one thing well. Forcing Writesonic to do all three is a mistake.

**Test two versions against each other.** Take your best Writesonic output and a human-edited version of a weaker variation. Run them as an A/B test in your store for 30 days. The data will tell you more than any opinion.

**Check out alternatives when you hit a wall.** If Writesonic is not clicking for a specific product category, [Jasper](https://jasper.ai/?via=chronostrail&utm_source=blog) and [Copy.ai](https://copy.ai/referral/chronostrail&utm_source=blog) use different models and sometimes produce better results for technical or luxury products. Having two tools available is smarter than going all-in on one.
